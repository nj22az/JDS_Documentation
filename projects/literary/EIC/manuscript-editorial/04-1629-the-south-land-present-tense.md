# 1629–1630: The South Land

> **1630**
>
> *They hired him after I warned them.*
>
> — Maria Mori

## I. The Packet

Rain strikes the Pelican shutters hard enough to bring 1603 back by sound alone.

Maggie is behind the counter when the packet arrives.

The Dutch master who carries it has brought it from Batavia to Amsterdam, Amsterdam to Gravesend and Gravesend upriver without putting it into a Company post. Oilskin outside. Sailcloth beneath. Paper folded inside that.

He sets it on the bar.

For the keeper.

Maggie looks at the outer sheet.

Four lines give the same direction in different hands.

Portuguese.

Dutch.

English.

Cantonese.

Tom is near the fire. Carter is discussing sailcloth with a man who has already regretted asking his opinion. Josiah occupies the same stool from which he has disliked most of the century.

Nobody gathers around the packet until Maggie opens it.

That is enough to gather them.

Inside is a letter in English.

The hand is controlled, upright, almost too exact.

Maggie reads the signature first.

Maria Mori.

Tom hears the name and turns.

Beneath the letter are two other sheets.

One is a Dutch warning dated before the sailing of the *Batavia*.

The other is a certified copy of the warning after a Company factor has written in its margin.

Maggie reads the added words.

*Experienced disciplinarian.*

*Useful.*

The Dutch master takes off his cap.

There is more, he says.

Maggie looks at him.

Begin where it begins.

He draws a breath.

Batavia harbour, he says. Before the ship sailed.

* * *

## II. A Name on the Muster

Maria Mori works behind a teak desk overlooking the harbour.

The desk is plain. Heavy. Too heavy to run with and too heavy for one man to carry away without assistance.

She chose it for both reasons.

Twenty-six years have passed since London.

She speaks Dutch fluently now. English exactly, though never casually. Portuguese without thinking. Cantonese when work requires it and when she chooses to hear something the European men in a room assume belongs only to themselves.

People come to her for translation, freight disputes, letters that need to survive two offices and rumours that become more expensive if they arrive late.

A clerk brings the *Batavia* muster because three entries require Portuguese rendering.

Maria reads all of it before beginning the three.

Old habit.

One name stops her.

*Silas Rook.*

Cornwall.

Heavy labour and ship discipline.

She reads the line twice.

The clerk notices.

You know him?

Yes.

Useful man?

Maria looks at him.

No.

She takes a clean sheet.

Her warning is short.

Rook is violent.

He is practised with a blade.

In Wapping in 1603 he murdered Matthew Bell, a clerk, then accused the only surviving witness and attempted to have others kill her for him.

He is not to be placed over unarmed passengers or labourers.

Maria signs her full name.

Then makes a copy.

The clerk watches the second sheet appear.

Necessary?

Yes.

He asks no second question.

Maria carries the first warning to the factor responsible for the fitting-out papers.

He is a careful man whose linen remains white in Batavia weather.

He reads the page.

Suspected in a murder, he says.

He confessed before witnesses.

Convicted?

No.

Maria waits.

The factor reads the muster line.

Rook is not signed as a soldier. Labour. Breaking work. Discipline where required.

That last word is the danger.

The factor takes up his pen.

Beside *violent* he writes:

*Experienced disciplinarian.*

Beside *practised with a blade*:

*Useful.*

Maria watches.

You have changed nothing I wrote.

No.

You have changed what it will do.

The factor sands the margin.

I have classified the information.

Maria puts her duplicate beside his page and checks each line.

Then folds it again.

The factor dislikes the copy. That is visible.

It remains hers.

On the quay three days later she sees Rook at the foot of the gangplank.

Age has thickened him. The beard is iron-grey in places. His shoulders remain excessive for the space around them.

He recognises her only when she says his name.

Silas.

His face searches hers.

Then finds the girl in the woman.

You.

Maria Mori.

He laughs.

Got yourself a better name.

I had it then.

Rook looks past her at the clerk waiting near the stores.

Heard you wrote against me.

I wrote about you.

And they kept me.

Yes.

His smile widens.

Useful, they said.

Maria says nothing.

Rook leans closer.

Paper likes me better now.

No, Maria says. Men do.

He goes aboard.

She watches until the crowd takes him.

Her warning remains in the office.

The duplicate remains with her.

Nothing else is in her control.

She has learned to distinguish that from doing nothing.

* * *

## III. Morning Reef

The *Batavia* strikes Morning Reef before dawn on 4 June 1629.

The reef enters the hull faster than orders can leave the deck.

People come up half dressed. Chests break loose. Water rises through spaces built to keep water out. Boats move between ship and low coral islands until the sea decides how much of the vessel it will permit to remain above it.

Hundreds survive the wreck.

Surviving the wreck proves to be only the first requirement.

There is little fresh water on the islands.

Commander Francisco Pelsaert takes the longboat with senior officers and others to search the coast, then continues for Batavia to obtain rescue.

He is gone for months.

In that absence Jeronimus Cornelisz builds another authority.

Food is controlled.

Weapons are controlled.

People are sent away in groups under reasons that sound practical until the people sent away fail to return.

Murder begins.

Rook does not create this order.

He recognises it.

That is worse in its own way.

A strong man willing to strike somebody smaller has value wherever fear is being turned into administration. Rook guards stores. Carries messages. Holds prisoners while other men question them. Goes in boats whose passengers do not all come back.

Some witnesses later say he killed.

Others say he held while others killed.

Several names attached to the worst acts die before anyone can question them.

Rook survives.

On another island Wiebbe Hayes and his soldiers survive too.

Cornelisz had sent them away expecting distance, thirst and bad ground to finish the problem.

Instead they find water.

They build with coral.

They organise watches.

When armed men come against them, they resist.

The fact that they remain alive becomes the fact on which everyone else's survival eventually turns.

When Pelsaert returns aboard the *Sardam*, Hayes's men reach him first.

They tell him what happened.

The mutiny collapses quickly after that.

The accounting takes longer.

* * *

## IV. The Island Court

Pelsaert and the *Sardam* council investigate on the islands.

Men are questioned.

Confessions are demanded.

Bodies and graves answer some questions better than the prisoners do.

Seven principal offenders are condemned and hanged there.

Jeronimus Cornelisz is among them.

Two younger offenders, Wouter Loos and Jan Pelgrom de Bye, receive another sentence.

They are to be put ashore on the mainland of the South Land.

They are given a small boat, provisions and goods intended for contact with the people of the coast. Written instructions go with them.

Their names enter the order.

Rook's does not.

The evidence against him is ugly and incomplete.

A carpenter says Rook held a sick man while another mutineer struck him.

A woman says Rook took her son toward the water and the boy never returned.

Another prisoner says Rook killed two people with his own hands, then changes the number under questioning.

Rook says Cornelisz ordered everything.

He says many things.

One of them is true.

The Company hired him after being warned what he was.

That warning is not on the island.

Maria is in Batavia.

Her duplicate is in Batavia.

Pelsaert's court can try what can be proved where the bodies lie.

Rook is not one of the seven executed men.

He is placed among the prisoners to be taken back for further judgment.

When the *Sardam* finally sails, Loos and Pelgrom are no longer aboard.

Rook is.

The South Land keeps the two men history names there.

Rook returns east in irons.

* * *

## V. Back to Batavia

The first thing Maria sees is the ship.

The second is how few people stand at its rail.

News comes ashore in pieces.

Wreck.

Mutiny.

Murder.

Hangings.

Two men left on the mainland.

Prisoners brought back.

Names follow later.

Maria goes to the office where the returning records are being copied.

She does not ask whether Rook survived.

She reads until she finds him.

*Silas Rook — remanded for further examination.*

No sentence.

No acquittal.

An open line.

The factor with the white cuffs is still alive and still careful.

Maria puts her duplicate warning on his desk.

He recognises it.

This again.

Yes.

The island evidence is uncertain.

The warning is not.

It proves you warned us about a man later implicated in a mutiny.

No.

The factor looks up.

It proves you were warned about a man later implicated in a mutiny.

He leans back.

That distinction will comfort nobody dead.

It is not for comfort.

Maria places the copy of his annotations beside the warning.

*Experienced disciplinarian.*

*Useful.*

The factor's face hardens.

You retained that.

Yes.

Why?

Because you changed what my words did without changing my words.

A clerk near the door stops writing.

The factor notices and dismisses him.

When the room is closed again he says, What do you want?

Maria answers immediately.

Attach both pages to Rook's examination.

For what purpose?

So nobody can write later that nobody knew.

That is all?

No.

Maria looks toward the shuttered window, beyond it to the harbour.

It is what I can require from you.

The factor studies her for a long time.

Then rings for the clerk.

The two pages are entered into the file.

Maria watches the clerk number them.

She does not smile.

* * *

## VI. Silas Rook

Rook is brought into an examination room two days later.

Not a grand court.

A table.

Three Company men.

A clerk.

Maria present because the file contains Portuguese and English statements requiring comparison.

Rook enters in irons.

He looks thinner than the man who boarded the *Batavia*.

Not smaller.

He sees Maria and laughs once.

Kept your paper dry.

Yes.

He sits when ordered.

The questions concern the islands.

Who gave orders.

Who controlled stores.

Who went in which boat.

Who returned.

Rook answers carefully when care helps him and roughly when roughness does.

Then one examiner reaches Maria's warning.

This was submitted before the voyage.

Rook glances at it.

I know.

How?

She told me.

And you sailed anyway.

They hired me anyway.

He looks directly at the factor's annotation.

Useful.

The clerk writes the word.

Rook sees him write it.

For the first time the satisfaction leaves his face.

The examiner asks whether Rook believes the Company bears responsibility for his acts because it employed him.

Rook smiles again, slower.

No. I bear mine. You bear yours.

Maria translates the sentence into Dutch.

The room is quiet after it.

One examiner asks her to repeat it.

She does.

Rook looks pleased with himself.

Maria does not give him the pleasure of denying the truth merely because he has spoken it.

The examination continues.

At the end Rook is removed.

He turns at the door.

What do you want them to do with me?

It is the first question he has asked her in twenty-six years.

Maria considers him.

In Wapping he wanted a room to kill her because his story was simpler than hers.

In Batavia he wants her to choose a punishment so he can place himself inside another simple story.

She refuses him both.

I wanted Bell to go home, she says.

Rook's expression changes.

That is not an answer.

No.

The guard takes him out.

Maria never sees him again.

* * *

## VII. What the File Says

Rook's file continues without Maria.

There are references to further inquiry.

A transfer order.

A note that one witness is unavailable.

A second note in another hand.

Then nothing Maria trusts enough to call an ending.

She could ask favours.

She could spend money.

She does neither.

Whatever happened to Silas Rook after the examination belongs first to Silas Rook.

She will not turn uncertainty into a punishment simply because uncertainty is available.

Instead she prepares a packet for London.

Her original warning copy.

The certified page showing what the factor wrote beside it.

A short English letter.

She writes:

*The man was employed after the warning. He later stood among the accused after the Batavia murders. I do not know his final account and will not invent it.*

Then another line.

*Keep these with Matthew Bell.*

She signs:

*Maria Mori.*

The packet goes by private hands.

* * *

## VIII. Wapping

The Dutch master finishes his account.

The Pelican has gone quiet around him.

Tom stands at the counter now.

Maggie reads Maria's English letter again.

Carter reaches for the certified factor's page.

Maggie puts one finger on it before he can take it.

Ask.

Carter looks at her.

May I?

She lifts her finger.

He reads the margin.

*Experienced disciplinarian.*

*Useful.*

His face does something Tom recognises and dislikes: the quick inward movement of a man seeing a commercial decision he understands too well.

Carter puts the page down.

Tom asks the Dutch master, Rook lived?

He came back from the islands.

After that?

The master shrugs.

The Batavia offices have more paper than certainty.

Tom looks at Maggie.

We let him go.

Maggie folds Maria's warning once along its existing crease.

Yes.

Everything after—

Belongs to the men who did it.

Tom's jaw tightens.

If we had held him—

Matthew might still have died, Maggie says. Maria might still have left. Rook might have hanged. Or not. You can spend the rest of your life building a cleaner night if you like.

Tom looks toward the shutters.

Rain strikes them again.

Maggie puts Maria's packet into the fault beneath the bar.

Beside Bell's page.

Beside Daniel Vale's.

Tom watches her hand disappear into the old oak and return empty.

Did she get justice? he asks.

Maggie looks at him.

Maria asked for the truth to stay attached to the paper.

That was not my question.

No, Maggie says. It was yours.

She picks up her rag and wipes a place on the counter that does not need wiping.

The Dutch sailors finally order ale.

Outside, the rain goes on.
