# Part II: The Price of a Man

## Chapter 6: Ten Pounds a Year

> **Wapping, 1691**
>
> *“A name kept where nobody can use it is only another burial.”*
>
> — Esther Finch

### I. The List Comes Back

The official list returns to the Pelican twelve days after Tanner sends it to Leadenhall Street.

It returns opened.

The Company seal has been cut from the lower edge without breaking the paper around it. A blue docket covers the back in three hands: received, examined, referred. Beneath those words somebody has written *attendance required* and the address of the Pelican as though East India House has discovered the inn by calculation.

The man carrying it is Edmund Pelling, a junior clerk with rain upon his hat and river mud upon shoes made for the City. He comes at ten in the morning expecting Tanner, a table and perhaps one witness.

Esther has provided twenty-three witnesses.

They occupy the settle, the hearth, the wall beneath the river window and every sound stool in the public room. Seven are women who have brought marriage lines, parish letters, dead men's promissory notes and children too young to understand why silence has been required of them. Three are men unable to work as they did before India. The rest are yard mates, neighbours, creditors and persons who claim no interest except having known somebody whom the sealed return has reduced to a mark.

Elias Finch sits at the short end of the bar with a carpenter's pencil and a sheet of sailmaker's paper. His right hand is swollen across the knuckles. He holds the pencil as he holds a marlinspike, between thumb and the first two fingers, and sharpens it whenever Pelling says something requiring patience.

Anders Blom has repaired the river shutter in exchange for his breakfast. His own tools remain under examination at Blackwall, so he has used Elias's plane, Elias's auger and three wedges cut from a broken chair. He stands beside the finished shutter testing the fastening while pretending not to listen.

Tanner occupies the chair nearest the fire.

He has worn his coat indoors since waking. His scaled boots rest squarely upon the boards. The room is warm enough for everybody else, but his hands remain closed beneath his arms and a small, regular tremor moves through his shoulders.

Pelling places the return upon the bar.

This was intended as a private examination.

Your letter said persons claiming under the return might attend, Esther says.

At East India House.

They attended here.

I cannot examine twenty-three persons in an alehouse.

Esther looks at the unopened account book beneath his arm.

You brought enough paper.

Pelling is not an unkind man. This makes him slower to recognise when kindness has ceased to matter. He looks at the widows, the maimed men, the children and Tanner shaking beside the fire. He removes his wet coat, folds it across the back of a chair and asks for ink.

Esther places an inkhorn before him.

One penny, she says.

For ink?

For the table. The ink comes with it.

He pays.

The Company examination begins.

### II. Persons Claiming

Pelling reads the first name.

Jonathan Hobbs, master gunner. Deceased in service. Effects recorded: one sea chest, working clothes in poor condition, one brass instrument.

The instrument is here, Tanner says.

Pelling looks towards the bar.

Esther does not retrieve it.

To whom was it delivered?

The house.

An inn cannot inherit.

It has done.

Pelling leaves a space beside the entry. The blank is the first thing he writes.

He proceeds to James Miller, gunner's mate.

Sarah Miller stands from the settle. She is a narrow woman in a brown wool gown polished at both elbows. A boy of nine rises with her until she presses him down again by the shoulder.

Pelling asks for her marriage line.

She gives him a folded paper from Ratcliff parish. It has been opened often enough to tear where her husband's name crosses the crease. Pelling lays two fingers upon the tear, compares the date with the return and asks when she last received an allotment from Miller's wages.

Three years this Christmas.

The return shows advances in Bombay after that date.

Then Bombay received them.

They are signed.

My husband made his J like a fish-hook.

Pelling turns the ledger so she may see.

The mark beside each advance is a cross.

Sarah Miller studies the page.

Then the cross had the money.

The room moves around that answer: a foot drawn beneath a stool, a child breathing through his nose, Elias's knife taking one more shaving from the pencil.

Tanner speaks without leaving the fire.

Miller could write his name. Slow, but he wrote it.

You witnessed these issues?

No.

Then I can enter only that the signature is disputed.

It is not a signature.

The form accepts a mark.

Miller did not make it.

Pelling looks at Tanner with the tired restraint of a clerk being asked to make paper distinguish between an authorised cross and a dead man's hand.

The return carries Miller upon the strength until Lady Day 1689.

He died before Christmas, Tanner says.

Sarah Miller turns.

You told me January.

I told you the letter would say January.

When?

Eighteenth December.

The truth reaches her without ceremony. She closes her eyes once. The boy beside her watches Tanner as though the correct arrangement of a man's face might reveal whether a father suffered.

Pelling puts his finger on the total.

If death occurred in December, the quarter's later wages cannot be claimed.

Sarah opens her eyes.

But his advances can?

Those must be examined separately.

So alive he owes you and dead you owe him less.

Pelling does not answer. He writes *date contested* in the margin and draws a box around it. The box is neat. Whatever happens to the claim, the handwriting has discharged its duty.

He reads the stoppages against Miller's account.

Clothing. Extra provisions during sickness. Medicines. Replacement of deficient equipment. Burial cloth and grave labour.

Sarah listens until the end.

Did he receive those things?

He was without boots, Tanner says. The surgeon had vinegar and a saw. I buried him myself.

With the Company's cloth?

With a piece of torn awning.

Pelling checks the column.

Then the burial charge may be erroneous.

May be?

The store issued twelve lengths that week.

To whom?

The return does not say.

Sarah looks at the figure charged against her husband's wages.

Then one of your dead men is wearing a very long shirt.

Even Pelling's mouth moves. The room does not laugh. A joke entered beneath a widow's name remains evidence.

The next two names belong to men Elias knew from Bell's yard.

Kit Boswell went out as a sawyer and Ned Gant as a shore carpenter. Neither appears by name. The return contains instead two European artificers attached to the northern work, one dead of fever and one absent after the withdrawal.

Which was which? Pelling asks.

That is what their wives have asked me, Elias says.

Were they Company servants?

They were hired through a contractor who hired through another contractor because the Company wanted carpenters without paying carpenter's rates.

Then their claims lie against the contractor.

The contractor died at sea.

Against his estate.

His estate is a coat, two buckles and a widow in Shadwell.

Pelling turns to Tanner.

Can you identify the men?

Tanner remembers a fever ward, two faces altered by heat, and Vane's return listing labour in groups because individual death made the column untidy.

Boswell had two fingers missing from the left hand, Elias says.

The sawyer, Tanner says. Died in the salt-house.

Cause?

Everything.

I require one cause.

Flux, then. If your box is small.

And Gant?

Tanner sees the Castle gate open after nine months. He sees men step into the emptied road. He sees a carpenter carried from a damaged Portuguese house with white lime across his hair.

Crushed chest after the withdrawal. Lived until evening.

Last words? Elias asks.

Pelling looks up.

Last words are not required.

His widow requires them, Elias says.

Tanner studies the fire.

Gant said the shore had been set wrong. He kept saying it though there was no shore beneath him.

Elias writes the sentence beside the name.

Pelling does not.

### III. Ten Pounds

By noon the return has produced no money.

It has produced six disputed dates, four missing certificates, two men placed beneath the wrong contractor and one widow whose husband appears as both dead and transferred upon separate pages.

Esther brings bread, cheese and small beer. Pelling says he cannot charge refreshments to the Company. Esther tells him nobody has offered to refresh him and gives his bread to Sarah Miller's boy.

The boy eats while looking at his father's cross.

Sarah asks about the deduction marked Poplar.

Pelling explains that a small levy upon Company wages supports relief for disabled seamen, widows and orphans. Application may be made to the committee. Service must be established, need certified and competing claims examined.

How much relief? she asks.

It varies.

How much can it vary to?

Pelling hesitates.

In a case such as yours, perhaps ten pounds a year.

The sum changes the room.

Ten pounds is large enough to imagine and too small to solve anything. It is rent for poor rooms, bread without meat, coal until the winter proves longer than the account. It is also more money than Sarah Miller presently possesses in the world.

For how many years? she asks.

During widowhood, subject to review.

Then I must remain poor and unmarried to prove my husband served you.

The fund is a charity, Mrs Miller.

He paid into it.

Contributions do not alone establish entitlement.

What do they establish?

Pelling looks down at the form.

Contribution.

Sarah laughs once. It is not a pleasant sound.

Esther takes Miguel's list from beneath the bar.

The paper is softer now. The fold crossing Miller's name has begun to whiten. She places it beside the official return and points to the line where Miguel recorded Ratcliff, Sarah, one son, brown door behind the cooperage.

Here is his family.

This is not an authorised record.

It found the right door.

That does not prove service.

The other list proves service and cannot find his name. This one finds his name and cannot prove service. How many true papers make one useful paper, Mr Pelling?

He examines Miguel's writing.

Who made this?

A priest in Bombay.

Under Company direction?

Under dying men's direction.

Pelling's expression changes. Not surrender. Recognition that refusal will now have to be chosen rather than assumed.

He copies Miguel's line onto a loose sheet.

This is not acceptance, he says.

No, Esther says. It is writing.

One by one, the house joins the two accounts.

Pelling reads the Company's number. Esther reads Miguel's name. Elias supplies a yard, trade or distinguishing injury. Tanner supplies the place where the person was last seen. Anders listens for men he may have carried aboard during the homeward loading and adds three descriptions in Dutch-accented English that Pelling reduces to fewer words.

The official return does not change.

Around it another document begins.

### IV. Witness

Tanner lasts until António Fernandes.

Pelling pronounces the name badly. Tanner corrects him, then corrects him again because the first correction has emerged through chattering teeth.

The room turns towards the fire.

Tanner's face is grey beneath the weathering. Sweat stands along his hairline while the rest of him shakes. When he tries to rise, the scaled boots hold their place and his body fails above them.

Elias reaches him first.

Anders takes the other arm. Together they lift Tanner without allowing the chair to fall. His weight surprises them. Fever has eaten the flesh from him without reducing the bones.

Pelling gathers the return away from the spilled mug.

Has he been examined by a surgeon?

In Bombay, Esther says.

I mean in London.

He has only just become ill in England.

Ague can return after a passage, Pelling says. It may not be infectious.

Then you may carry his legs.

Pelling looks at Tanner, at his own clean cuffs and at the stairs.

Anders carries the legs.

They put Tanner in the back room above the kitchen because its chimney gives warmth through the wall. Elias removes the scaled boots. The leather resists his swollen fingers, then releases each heel with a sound like wet timber parting from mud.

The old scars beneath surprise him.

What made those?

Tanner's eyes remain closed.

The world.

That is not a tool I know, Elias says.

It uses men.

Below, claimants continue speaking. Pelling's voice reads another entry. Esther's follows with another name.

Tanner opens his eyes.

Fernandes.

Alive when you left, Elias says.

Put that down.

They have.

Alive is not a settlement.

No, Elias says. But it is not dead.

Pelling appears in the doorway holding a form and Tanner's wage account.

I require his mark before I leave.

He has a name, Esther says behind him.

His signature, then.

For what?

Attendance, identification of casualties and acknowledgement that no payment has been made today.

The last part appears to Esther the first statement upon the page that twenty-three people can verify.

She takes the form, reads it from beginning to end and places a board across Tanner's knees. His hand shakes too badly to hold the quill. Elias closes both hands around Tanner's wrist until the point reaches the paper.

Tanner signs.

The name travels uphill.

Pelling sands it and waits.

East India House occasionally requires men who can identify foreign service papers, he says. There may be paid attendance when you recover.

Tanner's hand closes upon the blanket.

For reading lists?

For knowing when a list is wrong.

Esther looks at Pelling.

You have found the one thing the Company will pay him for.

Pelling puts the dry form inside his account book.

Knowledge has a rate like anything else.

He leaves Tanner's unsettled wage account on the stool.

### V. The Cup

The Pelican empties by degrees.

No claimant leaves satisfied. Three leave with copied forms. Two have been told which parish certificate to obtain. Sarah Miller leaves with Miguel's line in Pelling's hand and a direction to the Poplar committee. Ten pounds a year follows her into the rain like a promise too small to walk beside.

Pelling carries the official return back to the City.

Esther keeps the new sheet.

At dusk Tanner wakes to the smell of broth.

She sits beside the bed holding a cup made from red earthenware, one thumb over the crack in its rim. Steam reaches his face. The broth contains barley, onion and a little beef cut from the bone Elias bought for the house pot.

Tanner looks at it without taking it.

There are others downstairs.

They have eaten.

Miller's widow?

Went home.

With nothing.

With her husband's true date and less money because of it.

Esther holds the cup nearer.

Drink.

The room changes around Tanner.

For one instant the plaster wall becomes the Bombay storehouse and the rain at the Pelican's window becomes fists upon a locked grain door. He is standing between starving Company soldiers and sacks reserved for a strength return that includes dead men. Miller lies in the barracks with his leg black to the knee. Hobbs asks for powder. Vane's key hangs from Tanner's belt because the institution has chosen a man with hunger in his face to guard food from men whose hunger is worse.

He takes the cup but does not drink.

I kept stores from them.

Esther knows what he means without asking who *them* were.

Were the stores yours?

No.

Was the order?

No.

You kept it.

Tanner looks into the broth.

There was not enough.

There is never enough after a clerk has decided who is not present.

The sentence enters him more cleanly than blame.

He drinks.

The broth is thin, salted and hot enough to hurt the split places in his mouth. Esther waits until he finishes. Mercy would have allowed him to call the cup kindness. She does not.

When it is empty, she takes it and stands.

What happens to the names? Tanner asks.

They stay beneath the bar.

Then nothing happens.

Esther pauses at the door.

A name kept where nobody can use it is only another burial.

What will you do?

Make copies. Send them to the parishes. Give each woman the line that belongs to her.

The Company will not accept Miguel's list.

It accepted your memory when it wanted your signature.

She goes downstairs.

Through the floor Tanner hears the Pelican return to ordinary work: Elias drawing beer, Anders setting a wedge beneath the repaired shutter, Esther reading names while somebody else writes. The sounds resemble the scratching of Vane's ledger only from a distance.

Vane wrote to make men disappear correctly.

The house writes so they may become troublesome again.
