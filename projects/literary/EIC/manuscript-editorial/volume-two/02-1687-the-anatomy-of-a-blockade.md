# Part I: The Company’s War

## Chapter 2: The Anatomy of a Blockade

> **Bombay, 1687–early 1689**
>
> *“A cargo may spoil. Its valuation need not.”*
>
> — Ephraim Vane

### I. Prizes

In the Company’s letters, the war goes well.

The *Charles the Second* and *Modena* range beyond Bombay. Mughal and Siamese vessels are stopped, searched and taken. Other Company ships watch the mouth of the Surat River, intercept country craft and bring prize bottoms south under English colours. Every seizure acquires a number before its anchor is down.

In Bombay, Tanner watches the victories rot.

The first monsoon rain enters the old warehouse through the roof and leaves through the cargo. Raw silk swells inside its wrappings, dries badly and mats into grey ropes. Pepper absorbs the damp until the sacks become black bricks splitting at their seams. Indigo stains the floor around a broken chest. Coir mildews. Salt becomes water and finds a lower account.

The smell changes by the week.

At first the warehouse smells of distant profit: pepper, oil, damp cotton, wood and spices Tanner cannot name. Then comes mould. Then rancid coconut. Then rats dead behind bales too heavy to shift. By August, opening the door is an act assigned to soldiers.

Hawkins stands inside with a cloth tied over his mouth. The red sores on his hands have spread between the fingers. He uses a boat hook to pull apart a heap of coir and disturbs something alive beneath it.

The heap moves towards the wall.

Rats? Tanner asks.

Too many legs.

Then leave it for the prize court.

They back into the yard.

A newly captured snow is being warped towards the pier. She is perhaps sixty tons, high at the stern and foul beneath the waterline, with patched sails and a carved strip of red paint around her quarters. An English prize master stands on her deck wearing the former master’s red sash. He has a bottle in one hand and an inventory in the other.

The crew are not aboard.

They were removed at the anchorage and sent to the salt-house, where ninety-three masters, pilots, merchants, cooks, lascars and boys now occupy a building intended for dry fish and salt. The guards call every one of them boatman because the ration list has only one line.

The prize master comes ashore.

Four thousand coconuts, he says.

Hawkins looks towards the warehouse.

Where do you want them?

The man mistakes this for a serious question.

Under cover.

There is none.

Then make some.

With what?

The prize master gestures towards the vessel.

She carries coir.

Tanner takes the inventory. It values the snow, cargo and tackle at six hundred and eighty pounds.

How much fresh water aboard?

The prize master hesitates.

Two breakers.

For how many men?

Twenty-six now.

And when did you fill them?

Diu.

The answer is nearly a confession.

Tanner hands back the inventory.

Put one breaker ashore.

That water belongs to the prize.

The coconuts can drink it.

The man looks towards the Castle windows.

Mr Vane said the cargo is to arrive undiminished.

Then enter twenty-six dead men beneath it.

The prize master keeps both breakers aboard.

By evening six of his crew have come ashore with flux. The snow’s cargo remains intact.

### II. The Green Book

Ephraim Vane keeps the war in green calfskin.

The ledger lies open upon a folding desk on the council gallery. Its columns rule the shaded page with red lines: vessel, tonnage, origin, cargo, appraised value, seizure cost, custody, charge and expected disposition. The handwriting is narrow enough to make abundance from little.

Tanner stands before it with his cap beneath one arm.

Forty-two bottoms, Vane says. Estimated gross value, eighteen thousand pounds.

Eighteen thousand pounds of wet pepper and coconuts.

The Court does not purchase coconuts, Sergeant. It considers classes of goods.

The goods are spoiling.

Then their deterioration will be charged against the prize.

The factor enters a figure.

He has lost weight since the creek report. The black coat remains. A paler line circles his wrist where his cuff has been taken in. Nothing else about him acknowledges scarcity.

Tanner looks at the page.

The snow brought in this morning is entered at six hundred and eighty pounds.

That is the provisional value.

Who will pay it?

Its owner, if he wishes the vessel released. A buyer, if the court condemns it. The cargo interest, if it is redeemed separately. The prize fund, if charges absorb the proceeds.

And if no one comes?

Storage continues.

On rotting goods.

On goods under lawful custody.

Vane draws another line.

The brilliance of it reveals itself slowly. Every day the prizes fail to sell, they owe the Company more for being unsold. Guard fees, lighterage, wharfage, warehouse room, inspection, clerkage and damage surveys gather around cargo that has ceased to be worth carrying. When the charges equal the valuation, the Company may own nothing without admitting loss.

Your eighteen thousand pounds is eating itself, Tanner says.

All ventures incur cost.

There is no venture. We have stolen a midden.

Vane looks up.

Use that word again and I shall require you to prove it before the council.

Tanner gestures towards the watergate.

Open the door.

The factor closes the ledger.

You persist in confusing material condition with legal value. A bale may spoil. Its valuation need not.

And the men in the salt-house?

Detained personnel.

They have one water jar for twenty men.

Their subsistence is chargeable against local revenue.

There is no local revenue. The customs quay is empty.

Then it is deferred.

Until when?

Until settlement.

One of the junior writers has stopped copying. Vane turns his eyes towards him. The quill resumes.

Tanner leans closer to the green book.

Three vessels in this page belong to Abdul Ghafur’s house. The fourth carried cloth for your agent in Surat.

Vane’s face does not alter.

Which agent?

The one whose mark Hawkins saw beneath the new prize number.

Marks are frequently counterfeited.

Then seize his goods too.

The cargo was English-owned and travelled under local freight.

Mughal pass?

Commercial protection is not enemy ownership.

Tanner waits.

Vane has divided the sea more finely for his own cloth than the soldiers are permitted to divide it for anyone else.

Does Sir John know?

The cloth is entered correctly.

That was not my question.

No, Vane says. It was an attempt at purchase without money.

He opens the ledger again.

You have a troubling habit of looking at what lies beneath the number. A soldier enforces the Charter. A factor preserves its value. Do your work and I shall do mine.

From the yard comes a crash as the first basket of coconuts breaks through a rotten platform.

Neither man looks away.

### III. The Salt-House

The salt-house is long, low and badly named.

No salt remains inside. The Company removed the last sound stock for its own kitchens, swept the floor once and filled the building with prisoners. Salt persists in the walls. It draws water from the monsoon air and keeps the plaster sweating after the rain stops.

Tanner opens the door at midday.

Heat, breath and the smell of too many bodies come through it together. Men lie between old storage partitions or sit with their backs against damp walls. Most have stripped to cotton drawers. A cook from the captured snow fans a boy with a piece of sailcloth. Two elderly merchants share a dry strip of floor no wider than a ledger. Someone has arranged sandals in pairs beside the door, an order more precise than the Company’s list.

Miguel enters behind Tanner with a water jar.

The room rises towards it.

Stay down, Tanner says.

Nobody does.

The English guard at the door reaches for his staff. Tanner takes it from him before the first blow.

How much water this morning?

Full issue.

How much?

The guard looks at the jar.

One.

For ninety-three men?

That is what came.

Miguel says something into the room. Four answers come at once. He listens, points to an old pilot and receives the account again.

Two jars were brought, he says. One entered.

The guard’s face changes.

They lie.

Where is the other?

I do not know.

Tanner looks at the wet mark beneath the man’s own stool.

The guard has been selling cups through the rear vent to prisoners who still possess coin. It is a small corruption and therefore complete: a storekeeper signs two jars out, a clerk enters two, a soldier delivers one, and ninety-three thirsty men are invited to blame the heat.

Give me your keys.

Sergeant—

Keys.

Tanner opens the rear store. The missing jar stands behind a barrel with its lid tied down. Beside it lie three copper coins, a silver clasp and a child’s bracelet.

The guard is named Pritchard. He has a wife in Ratcliff and two months’ wages owing. He begins with that information as though Tanner has asked what water costs.

I meant to make it good.

With what?

My pay.

Your pay is paper.

Tanner carries the jar into the room himself.

Miguel takes the bracelet from the store and holds it up. A man near the far wall raises one hand. He is young, finely bearded and feverish. The bracelet belongs to his daughter in Cambay. He surrendered it yesterday for half a cup.

Pritchard watches it returned.

What happens to me?

What happened to the first jar. You are deferred.

Tanner puts him outside and assigns Hawkins to the door.

The prisoners drink in an order negotiated among themselves. First the sick, then the oldest, then the boys, then everyone else by vessel. No English officer devised it. No written rule is required. When one man tries to take a second cup, three others pull him back without striking him.

Miguel walks the rows with Vane’s list.

The paper has ninety-three lines and eighty-seven names. Six men are entered by use: cook, boy, Armenian, two pilots, blacksmith. One dead man remains present because nobody has yet authorised a change in strength. Two living men are absent because their names were mistaken for cargo marks.

The young man with the bracelet is called Hamid Rafiq.

He is clerk and nephew to Yusuf Rafiq of Surat. His employer owns part of the snow and none of the coconuts. The cargo belongs to forty-one interests spread between Cambay, Surat, Diu and Calicut, which makes Vane’s single valuation a feat achieved only by refusing to know anything.

Hamid speaks English.

You took a merchant vessel, he says.

I did not.

Your coat did.

Tanner accepts the correction.

Hamid points through the high vent towards the warehouse.

My uncle will buy the sound cargo.

Mr Vane says its owners must redeem it.

From whom?

The prize court.

Who owns the prize court?

The Company.

Who took the ship?

The Company.

Who sets the charge?

The Company.

Hamid closes his eyes.

Then you have sold it already.

The cook beside him laughs. Soon half the room is laughing, not loudly, at an English institution that has managed to conduct all sides of a transaction without finding a buyer.

Tanner waits for it to finish.

Will your uncle come?

If the message reaches him.

Miguel looks at Tanner.

No correspondence leaves the salt-house without examination.

That is Vane’s order.

Yes.

The priest takes one of the Company’s prisoner lists, turns it over and gives Hamid the stump of a quill.

Then examine this carefully when I bring it to you.

Hamid writes six lines.

Three days later, Yusuf Rafiq’s barge appears in the roadstead.

### IV. The Bill

The Surat merchants arrive during a clear interval in the rain.

There are three of them, attended by six servants, two armed men who surrender no weapons and Padre Miguel, who has been requested because the Company’s Gujarati writer has reported sick. They cross from the outer anchorage in a hired barge with no English flag.

The eldest is Yusuf Rafiq, a broker who represents several Surat houses and has lent money to men who later claimed to have governed him. His beard is dyed with henna. His white robe has remained white across the crossing, which Tanner considers a greater command of circumstance than anything yet achieved by the Company.

Yusuf steps onto the pier and stops beside the burst pepper sacks.

He asks Miguel a question.

Where is the sound pepper? the priest translates.

Under the wet.

Yusuf looks at Tanner.

That answer requires no translation.

The merchants are taken to the council chamber. Sir John receives them at the long teak table with Vane on his right and two sea captains on his left. No full coat of state, no wig, no ceremony larger than the room can carry. Child has learnt that sweat makes poor regalia.

Tanner stands at the door.

Yusuf places three documents on the table. The first lists vessels detained or missing. The second lists cargo owners. The third is a hundi, thin rag paper folded twice, drawn upon a Surat house whose mark even Vane recognises.

Miguel translates.

The merchants will pay fair harbour dues, necessary repair and the cost of returning crews. In exchange, the Company releases nine vessels and their undamaged cargoes.

Child reads the sum.

Thirty thousand rupees.

Paid on sight in Surat, Miguel says.

To whom?

To the Company’s order.

Child lays the bill down.

Your governor has detained English factors and seized English property.

Yusuf answers.

He says these goods belong to merchants, not the governor.

They sail under the emperor’s protection.

As English goods sail under your king’s protection.

Then your grievance is with the emperor.

Yusuf looks towards the open shutters and the captured snow below.

His reply takes longer. Miguel listens, asks one question in Portuguese and receives an answer in Gujarati.

He says an emperor may punish him. A merchant may cease doing business.

One sea captain laughs.

Vane does not.

The thirty thousand rupees would buy grain, powder, medicines and loyalty. It would also require the Company to treat the seizure as a bargain between merchants rather than an act of sovereign force. Tanner watches both calculations pass through Child’s face.

The Company’s claim is larger, Child says.

Yusuf waits.

One hundred thousand rupees for losses and insults, release of our servants, restoration of property and new trading terms.

The old broker folds his hands.

Miguel translates his answer.

The governor of Surat may discuss the governor of Surat. Yusuf Rafiq has come for nine ships.

They carry the Mughal’s passes.

They carry the property of men who will remember who returned it.

And if I refuse?

Yusuf draws the hundi back across the table.

Then the Company may eat its pepper.

No one moves.

The sentence is not insolence. It is arithmetic.

Child looks at Vane.

The factor’s eyes remain upon the bill as it disappears into Yusuf’s sleeve. For one instant his private understanding is visible: thirty thousand real rupees have just left a room containing eighteen thousand imaginary pounds.

The merchants depart with their papers and without their ships.

Vane opens the green ledger before their barge has cleared the pier.

Offer refused by local interests, he dictates. Claim thereby affirmed as insufficiently answered.

Tanner speaks from the door.

They did not say the claim was fair.

The junior writer waits.

Vane continues.

Insufficiently answered.

The quill moves.

### V. Back Bay

At night the blockade is made of small hungers.

Tanner patrols Back Bay with Hawkins, Miller and Jan van der Meer, a Dutch gunner who claims to have served every flag except the one currently paying him. Their order is to stop unlicensed boats, inspect cargo and prevent supplies reaching people designated hostile.

The order does not explain how to distinguish hostile rice.

They walk the wet curve of shore below the palms. Rain comes and goes. In the intervals, frogs fill the dark with machinery. Empty fishing huts lean behind the strand. Nets hang stiff where their owners left them after two boats were taken for carrying firewood without Company passes.

Miller drags his left foot more heavily now. The skin above the missing toes has opened again. He refuses the infirmary because men who enter it lose their duty ration before they lose their pulse.

A shuttered light shows once upon the water and vanishes.

Hawkins raises his matchlock.

Boat.

Tanner hears the small dip of paddles.

Hold the match down.

If they run?

The rice may escape.

The craft grounds fifty paces along the shore. Three figures lift baskets and move inland. Tanner waits until the last has reached the palms, then walks towards them.

Padre Miguel steps from behind a tree.

His cassock is wet to the knee. Beside him Rama Koli holds one end of a pole from which hang two lidded baskets. A woman Tanner does not know holds the other. The baskets contain limes, green leaves, onions, two jars of oil and a small quantity of rice.

Miller breathes through his teeth.

That is enough evidence to cure half the garrison.

Miguel looks at the matchlocks.

Will you arrest the onions first or proceed by weight?

The supplies are contraband.

Everything useful is.

Who paid?

The priest names three Portuguese families, a Parsi collector and one English widow whose husband’s wages remain in Vane’s accounts.

Tanner lifts a basket lid. The limes beneath are small and scarred.

Company stores have lime juice, Hawkins says.

Hobbs tried it, Miller answers. Said it tasted of a cask that once heard a lime described.

Jan van der Meer takes an onion.

Tanner closes the lid.

Carry them by the old burial ground. The west sentry sleeps after the second bell.

Hawkins turns.

Sergeant?

You saw an empty boat.

I saw—

You saw what you mean to enter when Vane asks.

The boy looks at Miller’s foot, the limes and the dark sea. His face changes by a small amount.

An empty boat.

They help carry the baskets.

After that, the patrol becomes a route. Firewood arrives beneath funeral matting. Betel travels inside powder returns. Fresh greens are entered as fodder for animals the Castle does not possess. Miguel keeps a list of households that can spare food. Tanner keeps no list.

Vane knows.

He begins assigning Tanner to Back Bay more frequently.

Neither man mentions why.

### VI. Strength

By the end of 1688, the blockade has turned around.

The Company still stops ships, but fewer ships enter reach. Mainland sellers keep away from the English ferries. Rice grows dear. Firewood is weighed before issue. Gun carriages disappear plank by plank into the armourer’s forge. Men break up casks and burn the staves, then discover that grain without casks attracts rats.

Hobbs’s gums swell around his teeth. He cleans each brass gun every morning and begins again after noon because salt appears while he watches.

You cannot polish the sea off an island, Tanner tells him.

Then I shall reduce it locally.

Miller goes to the infirmary in December.

His old wound is black at the edge and hot above it. The surgeon wants to take the foot. There is no clean saw. There is no laudanum. There is half a bottle of arrack Vane has entered for officers’ medicinal use.

Tanner takes it from the locked cupboard while Miguel stands in the doorway not seeing him.

The surgeon removes the foot.

Miller lives through the night and complains in the morning that the Company now owes him for four toes it never paid for. By the next evening he cannot remember Tanner’s name.

Above his pallet hangs a paper label.

**MILLER, J. — GUNNER’S MATE — RETAINED ON STRENGTH.**

Vane enters the infirmary with the green ledger and counts occupied beds.

Tanner blocks the next aisle.

You have your number.

I require names.

For pay?

For strength.

Miller opens his eyes.

He recognises the book.

Shroud, he says.

Vane looks towards him.

What?

He says you came yesterday to charge his shroud.

The factor turns a page.

Burial cloth is recoverable from accrued wages where sufficient.

Tanner’s hands close.

Vane observes them without stepping back.

If I omit the charge, the storekeeper bears it. If the storekeeper omits the cloth, the body is buried without one. You wish every cruelty to have one hand around its throat. Most are queues.

Miller laughs once. The sound becomes a cough.

Do not charge him, Tanner says.

On whose authority?

Mine.

You have no financial authority.

Then put the cloth against my pay.

Your pay is in arrears.

Put it behind the rest.

Vane makes the entry.

Miller dies before dawn.

They bury him beside the old Portuguese chapel in ground softened by rain. Miguel speaks the prayers. Hobbs attends with one hand against his mouth. Hawkins places Miller’s remaining shoe on the grave until Tanner removes it; leather cannot be spared for sentiment.

From the rise above the burial ground they can see north-east across the flats towards Sewri.

There are new fires beyond the water.

Not village hearths. Too evenly spaced. Too many appearing together and disappearing before daylight. Country boats have begun travelling north after dusk without cargo and returning lower in the water. The fishing families know something and have stopped selling the knowledge.

Hobbs studies the distant glow.

How many?

Tanner thinks of Rama’s hand pressed into dust.

Enough.

In the morning Vane’s strength return goes aboard the packet for England.

It records Miller as having died after completion of the December establishment, and therefore present for the quarter. His accrued wages remain subject to deductions. The garrison appears one man stronger than the burial ground.

The same dispatch reports prizes secured, values preserved, patrols maintained and no confirmed hostile concentration within the island’s northern approaches.

Every statement has a paper beneath it.

Beyond Sewri, men wait without one.
