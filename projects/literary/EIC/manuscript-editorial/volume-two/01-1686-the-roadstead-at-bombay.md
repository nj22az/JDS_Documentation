# Part I: The Company’s War

## Chapter 1: The Roadstead at Bombay

> **Bombay, late 1686**
>
> *“The tide does not read.”*
>
> — Padre Miguel de Sequeira

### I. The Mud

The mud of Bombay does not wash off.

It enters the weave of a man’s slops, dries to a grey salt scale beneath the sun and returns to paste the next time he wades ashore. It gets under a thumbnail and remains after the nail has grown. Men curse it in English, Portuguese, Marathi and three kinds of shipboard Dutch. The mud receives every opinion without improvement.

Tanner sits on an overturned teak cask beside the Castle watergate and works a blunt marlinspike beneath the crust on his knee.

He is thirty-two. His face has been burnt, peeled and burnt again until the skin resembles leather kept too near a galley fire. One tooth moves when he presses it with his tongue. His knees make a dry report when he stands, which Master Gunner Hobbs says is useful because an enemy may hear him coming and save powder by surrendering.

Hobbs is at the western battery with his shirt open and his remaining hair tied back in a piece of tarred line. He has taken the vent cover from a brass demi-culverin and is holding it towards the light as though expecting the sun to confess something.

Salt, he says.

Tanner keeps scraping.

The sea is chiefly salt.

The sea has the decency not to enter a touch-hole when the cover is sound.

Then blame the cover.

I am. It was entered as new.

Hobbs turns the leather over. The stitching has opened along two inches of the edge. Somebody has rubbed grease into it and hoped the grease would pass for repair.

New, Tanner says, is an entry in a book.

That is why books should defend the walls.

Beyond them, the roadstead shines so fiercely that every dark object acquires a rim of white. The *Modena* and the *Charles the Second* ride below the Castle, their yards bare for work and their boats travelling between ship and shore. They are large enough to make the Company’s ambitions appear reasonable from a distance. Nearer in, a dozen country craft move rice, coconuts, firewood, cotton and people with less noise and more regular profit.

The *Modena* is new, broad and expensive. Her name honours the Queen. Her seams do not care.

Men overside are paying them with hot pitch. A lascar stage hangs beneath the main chains, its planks rising and dropping on the harbour wash. The two men upon it keep their balance without interrupting their argument. Tanner cannot hear the words. He knows from their hands that one blames the oakum and the other the man who drove it.

Inside the Company’s house, clerks write.

Quills move from ink to paper and back again. Loose sand dries one page while another is copied. The letters will go by way of the Cape, cross two oceans and arrive in Leadenhall Street months after the weather, hunger and judgement they describe. There they will be read by men who can correct Bombay without smelling it.

A clerk in the upper gallery shakes a sheet too early. A black drop falls through the rail and lands beside Tanner’s shoe.

Hobbs looks down from the battery.

Enemy fire.

Tanner puts the marlinspike away.

The Company has spent the morning posting an order beside the gate. Every officer, factor and man who cannot read has stopped to read it. The notice gives Sir John Child’s authority in six lines of large hand and the consequences of obstructing it in twelve lines of small. Ships and boats carrying Mughal passes may be detained. Goods useful to an enemy may be seized pending examination. Any person resisting an officer of the Company may be treated as an enemy.

Nothing on the page states where examination ends and war begins.

Miller comes from the gate with betel reddening one corner of his mouth. Three toes are missing from his left foot, taken by an eighteen-pounder carriage on the voyage from St Helena. He walks as though every second pace belongs to somebody else.

It is signed, he says.

The wall told me.

They say the Bengal business has turned.

It has been turning since the letters left England.

Miller squats beside the cask. Across the yard a file of men carries powder barrels towards the magazine beneath awnings of wetted sailcloth. The barrels are marked with the Company’s heart. Two leak grey grains from their hoops.

Vane wants a boat’s crew after the evening gun.

For what?

Three teak boats in the creek. Mughal passes. Janjira guard.

How many?

Three.

Boats or guards?

Miller shifts the betel.

The order says three.

Tanner looks towards the upper windows.

Ephraim Vane stands inside one of them, thin and motionless in black wool. He has the complexion of a man who considers weather a disciplinary failure. At his shoulder a Company writer holds a ledger open. Before him stands a priest in a faded black cassock and a white cotton shawl, one brown hand moving as he speaks.

Padre Miguel de Sequeira is not attached to the Company, whatever Vane’s lists say. He serves a chapel beyond the English wall, hears confessions among families who were Portuguese before the English learnt the names of the islands, and translates when the Company has exhausted the people it pays to misunderstand.

Vane sees Tanner looking and lifts one finger.

The summons travels through glass.

Tanner rises. His knees give Hobbs’s warning.

Hobbs sets the failed vent cover beside the gun.

If he sends you where the water is shallow, ask whether the order floats.

The order will.

Paper does.

### II. Three Boats

Sir John Child has arranged the council chamber around a map.

The map is English, recent and expensive. Bombay is cleanly edged in black. Creeks take agreeable curves through green wash. Soundings sit in the water like facts dropped from a careful hand. The shoals do not move, the channels do not silt and no fisherman has placed a stake where the chartmaker omitted one.

Child stands at the table in linen and a light coat, one hand on the island he governs. He is thick through the chest, heavy at the jaw and greying beneath his own hair. Sweat has darkened the cloth beneath his arms, but nothing in his manner admits that the room is hot.

Ephraim Vane stands opposite him.

Vane is Senior Factor, First Class, and has the rare ability to make rank sound like a measurement of purity. He understands pepper, freight, customs, insurance, private trade and the exact point at which a debt becomes more convenient as a dispute. Men dislike him for his coldness and trust him with money for the same reason.

Padre Miguel waits near the door.

The priest is perhaps forty-five, perhaps older. The coast makes guesses about European age unreliable. His cassock has been patched at both elbows with cloth that does not quite agree with itself. He wears sandals. A small wooden cross rests against his chest beside a cord from which hang two keys, neither belonging to a church.

Tanner removes his cap.

You know the creek beyond Sion? Child asks.

The eastern water, Your Honour?

Up towards Thana.

At high water.

Child looks at the map.

It is water at low water also.

On the map.

Vane turns his head by a degree.

Tanner feels the warning and leaves it unused.

Child places a finger upon three small marks drawn beside the channel.

Three timber craft entered yesterday under passes issued in the Mughal’s name. Teak knees, mast pieces and squared lengths suitable for platforms. They are held under an escort from Janjira.

Held for whom?

That is what you will establish.

Miguel speaks for the first time.

The boatman did establish it.

Vane’s face remains still.

The boatman reported confused movements in several creeks.

He reported twenty fighting boats and three carrying timber.

In four languages.

In one language. I gave you the other three.

Child looks from Vane to the priest.

What fighting boats?

Miguel answers carefully.

Gallivats. Two ghorabs. Men from Janjira, or dressed as them. Matchlocks. Small guns on the larger craft.

Vane touches the open paper before him.

The informant saw the creek at dusk from among mangroves. He is a salt carrier, not a naval officer. His count cannot be relied upon.

But his three timber boats can?

Cargo is easier to count.

The exchange has been waiting for Tanner. He sees that now. Vane did not require a soldier to hear intelligence; he requires one to make a preferred part of it true.

Child moves his finger from the three marks to the mouth of the creek.

The timber may be intended for works commanding our northern water. If we permit a Mughal officer or the Sidi to close this channel, every stick of firewood and boatload of rice entering from that side becomes a favour granted to us.

He is not wrong.

Bombay cannot eat its charter. The island’s houses, kilns, cookfires, boatyards and batteries depend upon materials coming across water or down from country the English do not rule. The Castle is stone laid upon dependence.

You will take the great pinnace, Child continues. Nine men. Proceed on the flood, identify the timber and bring one vessel back. If resistance makes removal impracticable, burn the three.

Tanner looks at Miguel.

The priest does not rescue him with expression.

Your Honour, if the twenty are there—

They are not.

If ten are there.

Vane speaks.

The Company does not frame orders around every frightened estimate carried through a chapel.

Miguel’s hand closes over his keys.

The estimate was carried through your gate. You brought it to the chapel because your writer could not understand the man.

Vane ignores him.

Sergeant, a cautious reconnaissance that discovers nothing achieves nothing. You are to act.

Tanner studies the map. From the watergate to the creek mouth; the turn inside; the narrowing channel; the height of tide needed beneath a loaded pinnace. A flood takes them in. An ebb brings them out if they do not stay. Fire would announce them before the water turns.

What time were the craft seen?

Miguel answers.

At the last of yesterday’s ebb.

Were they grounded?

The timber boats, yes. The fighting craft lay in water.

Then somebody knows the channel better than this chart.

Child’s ruby ring touches Bombay.

You mistake knowledge for permission, Tanner. These waters are under the protection of His Majesty’s charter.

The creek is beside Portuguese land, crossed by Koli boats, used by Mughal merchants and watched by Janjira. Tanner does not list the owners. Ownership is the part of a chart most easily drawn.

He looks once more at the three ink marks.

When do I leave?

After moonset.

Vane closes his paper.

The boat will carry two firepots, twelve muskets, dry match, axes and a breaker of water. Expenditure of powder is to be accounted by charge.

Men?

What of them?

How are they accounted?

Vane looks at him.

By name, Sergeant.

It is almost a rebuke. Tanner has heard the casualty lists and knows better.

### III. What the Translation Said

Miguel catches him beneath the gallery.

The priest moves quickly when nobody important is watching. He takes Tanner through the bake yard, past a wall where split fish dry on cords, and into the strip of shade beside the cooper’s shed.

A man waits there with a basket of green limes.

He is narrow, barefoot and dressed in a cotton wrap hitched above the knee. A scar passes through the hair at his temple. He keeps his eyes on the gate, not Tanner. Two sentries can see him. Neither can see that half the limes have already been cut and packed again with their faces downward.

Rama Koli, Miguel says. He pilots salt boats.

The man says something.

He says he does not pilot English boats.

I did not ask him.

He says English questions become English orders if one waits.

Tanner looks in the basket. Beneath the top fruit lie six sound limes, a small clay jar and a roll of leaves tied in wet cloth.

For Hobbs?

And fourteen others whose gums bleed.

Company stores contain lime juice. Vane knows exactly how much.

Company stores contain an entry for lime juice. Miguel replaces the fruit.

Tanner looks towards the sentries.

Is that what the second key opens?

No. The second key is for a box where I keep the first key when Englishmen become curious.

Rama speaks again. Miguel listens without interrupting.

He was among the mangroves at the falling tide, the priest says. The timber boats are bait or payment. He does not know which. The fighting craft came from the south, not down from Thana. They entered without lights and placed two guns on the west bank.

How large?

Small enough to move. Large enough for your pinnace.

Why tell Vane?

He told Silva at the gate. Silva told Vane. Vane brought him to me after deciding what he had said.

Tanner understands the shape of it. If he finds only three timber boats, Vane’s intelligence is precise. If he finds a fleet, the fleet was concealed. If he returns without acting, the failure belongs to the soldier.

Rama reaches into the basket and removes one lime. With his thumbnail he draws a channel in the dust. A fork. A bar. Three marks beyond it. Then he presses his whole hand into the dust above them, fingers spread.

Twenty?

Rama looks at him.

Many, Miguel says.

Can he take us in?

The answer comes before the translation.

No.

For money?

No.

For protection?

Miguel gives Tanner a tired look.

From whom?

That is fair.

Rama wipes the chart away with his foot. He lifts the basket and goes towards the infirmary kitchen, carrying contraband health beneath a layer of spoiled fruit.

Miguel watches him.

If you meet those boats, he says, there may be no Sidi officer among them. There may be men hired by a merchant, a Mughal official, a Portuguese captain, or somebody wishing the English to fire first. Clothes travel more easily than loyalties.

Vane calls them Janjira.

Vane wants the noun to be finished.

And you?

I want you to come back. It makes me imprecise.

The evening gun sounds above them. Pigeons lift from the roof of the Company’s house and settle again before the echo has left the yard.

Miguel gives him one lime.

For your gums.

Hobbs needs it.

Then give it to Hobbs and tell your gums that charity begins with rank.

### IV. The Creek

They leave two hours after midnight.

Canvas is wound around the rowlocks. The firepots sit in sand-filled boxes beneath wet hides, each holding a small covered ember and the materials needed to turn three unguarded timber boats into a visible policy. Muskets lie between the thwarts. The men carry no lantern.

They do not require one.

Every stroke wakes green light in the harbour. The blades enter black water and leave pale fire curling behind them. Hawkins, seventeen and six weeks from Deptford, stares at the shining wake until Miller touches the back of his head.

The enemy is ahead.

What is that, then?

The sea telling them where we are.

Tanner sits in the sternsheets with the tiller beneath one hand. Padre Miguel sits beside him, his cassock exchanged for a dark cotton coat. He has refused a musket.

Why are you here? Tanner asks.

To tell you what they say.

We are not going to talk.

Then I shall tell you that.

The pinnace passes the darker shoulder of the island and works north-east. Behind them the roadstead becomes a scatter of riding lights. Ahead, the land breaks into low forms: mangrove, salt ground, the rise at Sion, Portuguese Salsette beyond water that belongs on different days to different authorities.

Hobbs has come as gunner because nobody else trusts the firepots. The lime makes a hard swelling in his cheek.

Fresh? Tanner asks.

Extremely.

You ate the rind.

It was entered with the fruit.

Miller smiles without showing his red teeth.

At the creek mouth the smell changes. Harbour tar and bilge give way to mud, leaf rot, fish, woodsmoke and the faint sweetness of something flowering beyond sight. The water narrows. Mangrove roots stand at the edge like black hands braced against the tide.

The flood is almost spent.

Tanner feels it through the tiller before he sees the change ashore. Floating husks no longer travel past them. The pinnace keeps way from the oars alone.

Hawkins whispers.

How long before it turns?

Long enough if we do not set fire to anything.

Hobbs adjusts the wet hide over the first pot.

An excellent use of firepots.

The first stake appears where the chart shows open water.

Tanner brings the tiller over. The boat misses it by a yard. Fifty paces on stands another, then a line of thin poles whose cut tops sit just above the surface. Fish trap, channel mark or barrier: the distinction depends upon who strikes one.

Rama’s dust chart returns to him. Fork. Bar. Three marks. A hand spread above them.

He steers towards the eastern branch.

Miller leans back from his oar.

Map says west.

The map is dry.

They enter beneath the mangroves.

No bird calls. That is the first certainty.

The second is a smell of slow-match. Faint sulphur, charcoal and saltpetre beneath the green rot.

Tanner raises one hand.

The oars stop together. The pinnace carries forward on silence.

Three timber boats lie grounded beyond the bend.

Their lateen yards are lowered. Squared teak fills them above the gunwales, dark lengths packed with wedges between. No crew shows. No cooking fire. No dog. The channel beside them is just deep enough for the pinnace to approach.

Hobbs uncovers one firepot.

Then the bank breathes.

A glow strengthens beneath a clay cover and is hidden again. Another answers further along. Slow-matches. Not two. Tanner counts seven before stopping.

Miguel touches his sleeve and points across the grounded craft.

Low shapes detach themselves from the mangrove shadow. One, three, six. Gallivats moored bow to stern inside the second channel, their oars run in, men lying below the bulwarks. Behind them rises the higher stern of a ghorab. A brass swivel catches starlight.

The intelligence was precise.

Three timber boats.

Everything omitted waits behind them.

Hawkins’s breath has become audible. Hobbs lowers the hide over the ember without being told.

A voice speaks from the dark.

Miguel listens.

He asks whose water we have entered.

Tell him the Company’s.

The priest turns his head.

Do you wish to come back?

Tell him we are a boat from Bombay and have lost the channel.

Miguel calls the answer in Marathi. Another voice responds, closer this time.

He says a boat that brings fire knows what it came to find.

Across the water, men rise.

They are not a single uniform body. Some wear quilted coats, some cotton wraps, some turbans, some caps. A few are dark-skinned Sidis; more are local sailors and hired matchlock men. The weapons are less orderly than Vane’s report and more real: matchlocks resting on rails, bows, tulwars, boarding spears. On the bank, the clay covers come off two slow-matches.

Hobbs speaks without moving his mouth.

If those guns are loaded with scrap, there will be enough of us left to identify the boat.

Tanner looks at the water.

The ebb has begun. A root at the bank shows a wet black finger above the surface.

The governor’s order is inside his coat, folded in oilskin. The powder account is in Miller’s pouch. Nine men wait for the word that will turn them into expenditure.

Tanner stands.

He takes the burning match from Hawkins and drops it over the side.

The coal gives one red turn beneath the water and dies.

Cover both pots.

Hobbs does.

Ship oars. Back her quietly.

Nobody argues until the first stroke.

Hawkins’s voice breaks.

The order—

Is six miles south in a dry room.

Sir John will put us in irons.

Then row while your hands are free.

The pinnace moves astern. Its bow remains towards the waiting craft. Miguel calls that they are leaving. No answer comes.

One of the bank matches lowers to a touch-hole.

Tanner sees the small deliberate movement and understands that command exists on the other side too. Somebody there has decided not yet.

The pinnace reaches the fork. Tanner turns her with the ebb. A shot would take them lengthwise now. None comes.

The dark men, boats and guns remain where they are, preserving their own account of what happened.

Only when the mangroves close behind them does Hawkins breathe properly.

Why did they let us go?

Miguel answers.

Perhaps they did not come for ten wet Englishmen.

What did they come for?

The priest looks south, towards the unseen Castle.

To see who sent them.

### V. Unfavourable Tide

They reach the watergate after sunrise with both firepots, every musket charge and all ten men.

Sir John receives these items as evidence of failure.

He stands in the council chamber without his coat. Vane has already placed the map, order and powder account upon the table. Tanner’s men wait in the lower yard under guard. Nobody calls it arrest because the irons have not yet been brought.

You found the timber? Child asks.

Yes, Your Honour.

And did not burn it.

There were armed craft in both channels and guns ashore.

How many?

Enough.

That is not a return.

Twenty or more. Two ghorabs that I saw. Gallivats behind them. Seven matches on the western bank, perhaps more.

Vane’s quill pauses.

Perhaps?

I did not go ashore to count.

You were ordered to act, Child says.

I acted. I brought back the boat.

The muscles at Child’s jaw tighten. His anger is not madness and Tanner refuses himself that comfort. The governor understands the Company’s weakness. That is why he cannot permit weakness to be visible. Every unburnt timber boat is a fact that may travel to Surat, London or Aurangzeb’s camp.

Ten men and a pinnace, Child says. Against country boats.

Against men waiting for ten men and a pinnace.

Vane lifts the report.

Padre Miguel’s translation was not considered reliable.

It was exact.

The priest is outside the Company’s discipline.

The creek is outside it too.

Vane puts down the quill.

That sentence may be entered as refusal of authority.

Enter the depth of water first.

Child walks to the window. Below, Bombay works because people who have not read his order carry water, mend roofs, unload rice, scrape hulls and open stalls. The *Modena* lies in the roadstead, large enough to promise force and too deep to enter the creek.

For a time the room contains only the harbour sounds.

Child turns.

Were you seen?

Yes.

Did you fire?

No.

Did they?

No.

Did you identify command?

No.

Then you cannot establish that these were the Sidi’s men.

No.

Nor that the timber was intended against Bombay.

No.

The governor looks at Vane.

The factor understands before Tanner does.

An engagement that has not occurred may still be made useful.

Vane draws a fresh sheet towards him. He writes the date, the pinnace, the creek and the purpose. His hand is narrow and entirely legible.

Reconnaissance prevented from closing with suspected supply craft by an earlier-than-calculated turn of tide and uncertain channel. Enemy affiliation unconfirmed. Stores returned complete. Further observation required.

Tanner watches disobedience become hydrography.

The tide did not turn early, he says.

Vane sands the page.

According to whose table?

Mine.

You have a table?

I have eyes.

Then we cannot enclose them with the report.

Child returns to the map.

You will not be put in irons, Tanner. You will return to ordinary duty. You will speak of no fleet until its ownership is established.

And if it comes here first?

Then we shall establish it from the walls.

The interview is over.

Outside, Hobbs waits beside the failing vent cover. Miguel has delivered the remaining limes and vanished through the gate before Vane can ask which account bought them.

Miller looks at Tanner’s wrists.

Nothing?

Nothing entered.

Across the roadstead a bell sounds aboard the *Charles the Second*. Boats put off from her side. On the eastern water the tide continues to fall at the hour it was always going to fall, exposing grey banks, fish stakes and the routes by which an island may be approached.

Tanner takes the marlinspike from his belt and sits again on the teak cask.

Above him the clerks copy Vane’s report.

By the next ship, Leadenhall Street will learn that the Company sent a pinnace into a doubtful creek, recovered every man and charge, and wisely postponed action because the water behaved unexpectedly.

The report will be true in every item except the thing that happened.

Tanner presses the spike beneath the mud on his knee.

It comes away in one hard piece.

The stain remains.
