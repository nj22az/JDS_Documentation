# Part I: The Company’s War

## Chapter 4: The Salt-House Siege

> **Bombay, 1689**
>
> *“Scarcity is only disorder until a clerk gives it columns.”*
>
> — Ephraim Vane

### I. Issues

The siege enters the Castle by measure.

At first the people in the yard flinch at every gun. By March they distinguish the sound of a charge from Dongri from the smaller pieces placed in stone houses nearer the wall. By April children look up only when a ball strikes. By the first monsoon rain, families have learnt which corners remain dry, which arches throw splinters and how long a man may stand at the cistern before a guard decides he is stealing.

Vane gives each necessity a column.

Grain is issued by household.

Water is issued by vessel, company or civil department.

Powder is issued by gun.

Medicinal spirits are issued by rank.

Burial cloth is issued only after death has been certified by a man who can write.

The people who entered without a category must attach themselves to one or receive nothing.

The old salt-house becomes the largest category.

Its imprisoned crews remain along the eastern wall. Wounded militia occupy the centre. Families from Mazagaon lie beneath the high vents, where air sometimes moves. Men with fever are placed near the rear door until the rear door is barred because three walk out during rain and do not return.

The salt draws water from the walls. The floor never dries. Straw blackens within days and is turned before it is replaced because there is no replacement.

Padre Miguel walks between the pallets with a board upon which he has written names in Portuguese, Gujarati, Marathi and English. Sillitoe follows with Vane’s official return.

The two lists disagree about almost everyone.

Miguel stops beside an Indo-Portuguese gunner whose shoulder has been opened by stone.

António Fernandes.

Sillitoe consults his sheet.

Francis, Anthony. Missing from Mazagaon.

He is here.

Then he cannot be missing.

Correct it.

Sillitoe hesitates.

If I restore him to strength, his ration resumes.

He is eating.

From whose issue?

Miguel points to the man’s wife.

Hers.

The writer looks down at the wounded gunner and discovers the danger in accuracy. A name restored to life consumes rice. A man kept missing costs nothing.

He writes a small mark beside the line but does not alter the category.

Tanner watches from the door.

He has been assigned to grain.

The order is simple: no store is to be opened except upon a signed issue. The grain room contains twenty-eight sound bags, eleven damaged bags, three open bins and more armed men than any other point inside the wall.

The people outside it have begun to smell the rice through stone.

Hawkins stands opposite Tanner with his matchlock grounded. The sores on his hands have healed into pale seams. His gums have not. He keeps his mouth closed because two teeth move when he speaks.

How many bags? Tanner asks.

Hawkins gives the official answer.

Forty-two.

How many can be eaten?

Twenty-eight.

Then we guard twenty-eight.

Vane appears behind them.

You guard forty-two.

Men cannot eat an entry.

Entries determine issue.

Rot determines it sooner.

Vane holds out a ration paper. It assigns one bag to the President’s house, one to officers, three to the hospital, four to artillery and the remainder by company and household.

The President’s house still has rice.

The President’s establishment has an issue.

The hospital has two hundred people.

It has three bags.

Hobbs has twelve gunners.

Artillery includes labourers.

Tanner reads the paper again.

Every line can be defended separately. Together they starve the same people.

Who decided this?

The council.

Which man?

The council is not a man.

It eats better than one.

Vane waits until Tanner folds the paper.

An issue is authority made visible, Sergeant. If you permit private judgement at the door, the store will be empty by evening.

And if I do not?

It will be empty later.

There is no cruelty in Vane’s answer. Cruelty would acknowledge choice.

### II. The Wall

The Sidi’s men do not waste themselves against stone.

They occupy the buildings the Company failed to clear, cut approaches through gardens and raise works of earth, timber and baskets. On Dongri Hill they place guns where the English can see smoke before they hear the report. Nearer batteries grow by night. Each morning reveals another line in the ground.

The Castle answers with whatever will still move.

Hobbs builds protective banks from palm trunks, dirt and ruined furniture. Teak chests become gun platforms. Doors become splinter screens. A carved bedstead from the governor’s former guest room is sawn into wedges for an eighteen-pounder carriage.

Vane objects to the bed.

It is entered as furnished accommodation.

Hobbs drives a wedge beneath the rear truck.

Now it is entered beneath number three gun.

The gun has developed three defects.

Its vent is burning wider. The left rear truck binds on its axle. One breeching bolt has begun to pull from the wall.

Hobbs treats each as an enemy with a name.

Tanner understands machinery better than he understands councils. A cracked cheek cannot be persuaded by rank. A hot vent will not accept a favourable report. Iron expands, wood swells, rope chafes and powder fouls according to laws that require no seal.

He and Hobbs reduce the charge, shift the recoil bed and serve the breeching through a second point. The gun loses range and stops trying to tear itself from the parapet.

Captain St John sees the smaller powder measure.

Who authorised this?

The vent, Hobbs says.

I ordered full service charges.

Then stand behind it.

St John looks at Tanner.

Sergeant, place the regulation charge.

Tanner holds up the old measure. A hairline split runs from rim to base where heat has opened the solder.

This regulation measure leaks.

Use another.

The other belongs to a sound gun.

St John’s jaw tightens.

The enemy is building within range.

That is why we need the piece tomorrow.

The captain can order a gun fired. He cannot order bronze not to burst.

He leaves.

Hobbs spits blood over the wall.

There. Another officer repelled.

They fire twice before noon. The first ball tears through a new gabion and throws earth across the work. The second strikes stone, breaks and returns fragments towards the Castle.

After each shot, Hobbs listens to the barrel cooling.

Tanner once asks what he hears.

Expense.

The answer pleases him for half a day.

Then a ball from Dongri passes over the parapet and enters the yard. It strikes the grain-room lintel, fills the doorway with lime and cuts one guard across the throat without touching the bags.

People rush the open door.

Hawkins lowers his match.

Tanner strikes the pan shut.

Not into them.

They will take the store.

Then use the stock.

He and Hawkins hold their matchlocks crosswise and push. Men who have stood at the same wall strike them with bowls, sticks and bare hands. A woman bites Hawkins through the sleeve. A wounded militia man tries to crawl beneath Tanner’s legs. Someone shouts that the officers have a private store.

They are right.

The doorway narrows under bodies.

Tanner reaches behind him, cuts the cord on one damaged sack and drags it into the yard. Grey rice spills through a split dark with damp.

Take one bowl, he says. Take two and the door closes.

Authority disappears.

Anandi restores it.

She is the woman who carried Rama’s basket through Back Bay and reached the gate with a boy upon her hip. Her name entered no Company paper before the siege. Now she stands over the spilled grain with a broken oar and strikes the first man who fills his bowl twice.

One, she says in English.

It is almost the only English word Tanner has heard from her.

The crowd forms a line.

Hawkins touches the bite in his sleeve.

Is she Company?

Not unless Vane means to pay her.

They issue the sack.

When Vane learns of it, he does not accuse Tanner of theft. He subtracts the weight from the next salt-house ration.

Mercy has moved the shortage.

It has not removed it.

### III. Departures

The monsoon closes the sea and opens the Castle.

Rain comes sideways across the wall. It enters vents, magazines, bedding and wounds. The Sidi’s trenches collapse and are dug again. English palm bulwarks slump into the ditch. Powder must be dried in pans when the sun appears and hidden when the clouds return.

The water inside the cistern rises.

So does the filth.

Men begin to leave.

Some climb the western wall at night and trust the rocks. Some bribe boatmen. Some walk out during agreed exchanges and fail to walk back. The Sidi’s commanders accept soldiers who bring weapons, information or skill. A few converts take new names. Others change nothing except employer.

Jan van der Meer goes in rain.

He has claimed for two years to have served every flag except the one paying him. The joke becomes a method.

At morning watch Tanner finds Jan’s matchlock beneath a dry overhang, cleaned, unloaded and wrapped in cloth. His shoes are gone. Two shallow heel marks lead towards the breach before rain erases them.

Hawkins calls it desertion.

Hobbs calls it punctual.

Vane calls it an account.

He opens the green ledger at a page headed Absences, Deaths and Desertions and asks Tanner for the hour.

Between bells.

Which bells?

The ones we can no longer hear because the chapel is outside the wall.

Was the weapon retained?

Yes.

Powder?

No issue missing.

Personal effects?

Blanket, one spare shirt and shoes.

Then the estate owes the value of the blanket.

He is alive.

Then it is not yet an estate.

Vane changes the heading.

Absences, Deaths, Desertions and Persons Otherwise Removed from Establishment.

The words require a smaller hand.

Does his pay go to his wife? Hawkins asks.

Did he have one?

He said there was a woman in Rotterdam.

Did he give a name?

No.

Then the Company cannot remit to a city.

Hawkins looks at Tanner.

The boy who arrived expecting calico profit has learnt enough to recognise theft after it has been ruled.

Vane enters the blanket.

By September, more names occupy the heading than the active list.

### IV. What Enters

Miguel’s route survives because it never becomes one route.

A fishing canoe comes under the wall at high water with limes beneath bait. A Portuguese cart approaches during an exchange with lamp oil hidden in funeral jars. Children carry onions in their clothing. Men in the Sidi’s lines sell information to families inside and men inside sell powder to people who may use it against them.

No side controls hunger completely.

Tanner receives the loads at the sea gate.

Every basket requires a choice.

The boat that carries two water jars cannot carry the same weight in grain. The space used by limes cannot be used by match cord. A wounded gunner and a fevered child require the same clean cloth. The priest brings no abundance, only alternatives small enough to hold.

One night he arrives with a covered pot and six limes.

The pot contains goat broth.

For whom? Tanner asks.

Miguel does not answer.

For whom?

The priest looks towards the salt-house.

Anandi’s boy has fever.

And the limes?

Hobbs.

Tanner lifts the pot.

It is light enough to settle nothing.

Give the boy the broth.

Miguel waits.

Give Hobbs four limes. Fernandes and Hawkins share one. Keep one.

For whom?

You will know after everyone has asked.

The priest takes the load.

Tanner has made a fair division only if every need is equal, which it is not.

The boy dies before dawn.

Anandi does not accuse him. She washes the pot, returns it to Miguel and resumes carrying water in the salt-house. Her silence is worse than judgement because Tanner cannot argue with it.

Hobbs eats one lime and gives the other three to his gun crew.

The next morning he loses another tooth.

### V. The Sea Gate

The monsoon does not stop the small war upon the water.

It shortens it.

Boats that once crossed the harbour by sail now move between squalls, using twenty minutes of clear air as if it were a road. Gallivats wait behind the eastern point. Fishing canoes lie beneath branches until the rain hides them. The Company keeps one longboat at the sea gate with six oars, no sail and a swivel whose iron bed has swollen into its mounting.

The boat is listed as serviceable.

Hobbs drives the swivel free with a mallet.

There, he says. The ledger may fire it now.

Near dusk a country boat appears from the south with a white rag at its masthead.

It rides too low.

Miguel stands in the bow. Behind him sit two Koli boatmen, four jars of water, twelve baskets covered in wet matting and a carpenter named Dias who has been outside the Castle since the gate closed. The current takes them past the proper approach. They lower a stone tied to coir, catch bottom and swing towards the wall.

Hawkins signals with a shuttered lantern.

The answer comes twice.

The sea gate has a timber boom worked by chain through a windlass inside the arch. Its outer end has swollen. Six men strain upon the bars and lift it only two feet before the chain stops.

Again, Tanner says.

The men lean.

The pawl clicks once.

Vane arrives carrying the gate issue book beneath his coat.

What cargo?

Water, food and a carpenter.

Under whose pass?

Miguel’s.

That is not a Company pass.

It is the only one that arrived.

Vane looks through the rain at the low boat.

The boom is not to be opened for unexamined cargo. We have lost powder through this gate.

Then examine it inside.

If the cargo contains powder for the enemy?

Then they have chosen an expensive way to return it.

A lookout above them strikes the alarm bell once.

Two long boats have cleared the eastern rain. They are low in the water, each pulled by paired oars. No flag is visible. Men crouch between the rowers. The leading boat carries a brass swivel at the bow.

Tanner takes the issue book from Vane, places it upon a dry ledge and puts the factor’s hand over it.

Keep that dry.

He adds four more men to the windlass. The chain rises another foot. The country boat pulls its stone, comes beneath the lifted timber and grounds upon the sill.

Miguel jumps into water to his waist.

Lighten her.

The boatmen throw the first baskets overboard. Limes rise and turn in the dirty water. A jar follows, strikes the wall and becomes a brief sheet of clean water upon the tide.

The boat remains caught.

Dias points below the stern. Their coir anchor line has drawn under the raised boom and jammed between timber and stone. The current tightens it. The more the windlass lifts, the deeper the stern is pulled.

The approaching swivel fires.

Its ball strikes high upon the arch and fills the gate with stone dust. Hawkins answers with the Castle piece. The swollen mounting turns half an inch, no more. His shot crosses the water and disappears into rain.

Tanner wades out.

The scaled boots fill at once and become weights upon his legs. He reaches the stern, feels beneath the surface and finds the coir line hard as timber. Dias leans over with a knife.

Do not cut it, the boatman says. The stone is all we have.

Tanner looks at the two boats closing through rain.

The stone is outside.

So are we.

Another swivel speaks. A ball passes through the country boat from side to side. Water enters in two clean streams. One of the Koli men folds over the thwart with both hands pressed beneath his ribs.

Tanner takes Dias’s knife and cuts.

The line parts strand by strand. The stern rises. Men upon the inner side seize the boat with hooks and drag it through as the windlass crew lowers the boom behind it.

The pursuing boats turn away before the Castle swivel can be forced to bear.

Inside the arch, everything becomes weight.

One jar has broken and another is lost. Three baskets have gone overboard. Two contain limes, one onions. Of the remaining nine, five hold rice, two lamp oil, one linen and one the private letters of people who believed paper might enter where they could not.

The wounded boatman dies while Sillitoe writes the count.

His companion gives the name twice.

Sillitoe asks for an employer.

Miguel answers before Tanner can.

The boat.

That is not an establishment.

It employed him until this evening.

Vane returns for the issue book. Rain has marked the cover where his hand failed to protect it.

The lost cargo must be charged to somebody, he says.

Charge the tide.

The tide has no account.

Then it is the only innocent party in Bombay.

Dias kneels beside the windlass, examines the chain and places two fingers into a crack running through the outer bearing.

This opens once more, he says.

Once?

If the next boat is lucky.

Tanner looks at the shattered jar, the dead man and the boom the Company still calls serviceable.

Make it twice.

By morning Dias has cut a new bearing from the last sound piece of the governor’s bedstead.

Vane records it as a repair to furnished accommodation.

### VI. Hobbs’s Gun

By November, Hobbs climbs the northern stair upon hands and knees.

He refuses to remain below because the number-three gun has begun to jump left upon recoil. Tanner finds the cause in the forward truck: the edge has split along the grain and sheds a thin crescent each time it rolls.

There is no replacement wheel.

They rotate the damaged truck, bind it with two iron straps taken from a provision chest and bed the carriage upon greased timber. It will not traverse freely. It will fire in one direction.

Hobbs studies the enemy work.

One direction is enough if they are courteous.

His left leg is swollen beneath the knee. Dark fluid marks his stocking. His gums are black around the remaining teeth, and his skin has acquired the yellow-grey colour of old sailcloth.

Go below, Tanner says.

And have Miguel list me beside the children?

You cannot serve the piece.

I can insult anyone who does.

The Sidi’s battery opens before noon.

The first ball strikes the outer bank. The second carries away two feet of parapet. The third enters through the embrasure, hits the right cheek of the carriage and tears a timber splinter through Fernandes’s forearm.

Hobbs is knocked from his stool.

Tanner reaches him.

The old gunner pushes him away.

Sponge.

You are hit.

Sponge the piece.

The barrel has fired once and stands loaded with the second charge. Burning fragments from the shattered screen lie around the muzzle. If a fragment remains in the bore, the cartridge may take while men ram.

Tanner worms, sponges and feels the wet head catch.

Again, Hobbs says.

The second pass comes out with a strip of smoking palm fibre twisted around the sponge.

Fernandes laughs through clenched teeth.

Hobbs points his three-fingered hand.

Elevate one degree. Reduced charge. Wait for the smoke.

The men serve the gun.

When the enemy battery appears between its own discharges, Tanner gives the word. The eighteen-pounder runs back upon the damaged carriage. The bound truck holds. Through the smoke comes the sound of timber breaking beyond the wall.

Hobbs smiles.

Charge that to my wages.

They carry him below after sunset.

He lies in the salt-house against a partition that still smells faintly of old fish. Miguel removes the stocking. Nobody discusses the leg after seeing it.

Hobbs keeps his brass quadrant beneath his hand.

Tanner sits beside him.

You were right about the charge.

I am usually right when nearly dead.

Where does the quadrant go?

Hobbs opens his eyes.

Wapping Wall. The Pelican.

To whom?

Any old liar who says he knew me under Blake.

There will be twenty.

Then make them prove it. Ask which gun I lost three fingers to.

Which gun?

I never told any of them.

Tanner almost laughs.

Hobbs closes his eyes.

Give it to the house, he says. Sailors leave enough debts there. It may as well have one sound instrument.

He dies during the middle watch.

No gun marks it. Powder cannot be spent upon private grief.

Miguel adds his name to the board.

Sillitoe enters Master Gunner Jonathan Hobbs, deceased, effects pending.

Vane carries him at full rate to the end of the accounting month.

### VII. Foreign Hide

The dead leave more property than space.

Their sea chests stand beneath the salt-house eaves until rain opens the joints. Clothing is boiled, burned or issued. Tools pass directly to hands that know them. Belts become slings. Shoes are paired by size rather than ownership.

Sillitoe conducts the inventory with a rag over his mouth.

Hobbs leaves one shirt, a powder horn, two vent prickers, a folding rule, the brass quadrant and wages that exist only as a claim against the Company.

The quadrant goes beneath Tanner’s coat before Vane can value it.

Beside Hobbs’s effects lies a bundle marked VAN HORN.

No one agrees what happened to Van Horn.

One man says he died before the monsoon. Another says he went over the wall with Jan. Hawkins remembers carrying him from Mazagaon with a cut behind the ear. Miguel has no Van Horn upon his burial board.

The bundle contains a knife without a blade, a wooden spoon, sail needles, three foreign coins pierced for a cord and a pair of high boots.

Sillitoe unfolds them.

They are not Spanish riding boots and have never belonged in a carpeted room.

The uppers are made from dark scaled hide, broadly cut and repeatedly patched. Their first maker understood feet less well than weather. A later hand has opened the seams, added a strip at each instep and closed them with tarred sail-twine driven through old holes. The soles are thick, uneven and sound.

Sillitoe touches one scale with his quill.

What hide is that?

Tanner bends it beneath his thumb.

Foreign.

From where?

Foreign is already a place in your book.

The writer looks at his form.

Condition?

Tanner’s left shoe has opened along the welt. Mud shows through it. The right sole is held by cord beneath the arch.

Unserviceable, he says.

Sillitoe writes:

> One pair high boots, foreign hide, unserviceable.

He assigns no value.

Tanner takes them to the cistern, rinses the salt from the soles and leaves them beneath the eaves until morning. No initials emerge. No maker’s stamp. No port mark. The scaled hide sheds water differently from cow leather and holds old scars between the patches.

He asks no question the missing owner cannot answer.

The left boot fits first.

The right is tight across the instep. He wets it, works the leather with both hands and pulls until his heel settles. The tarred stitching creaks but does not part.

He stands.

For the first time in months, stone does not enter through his soles.

Vane sees the boots when Tanner returns to the northern work.

Those were inventoried?

Foreign hide. Unserviceable.

Vane considers the wet scale pattern, the broad cuffs and the feet now occupying them.

Then no charge arises.

He continues towards the council room.

The book has made the boots worthless.

That is another kind of permission.

Tanner reaches number three gun. Fernandes has taken Hobbs’s stool. Hawkins holds the reduced powder measure. The repaired truck remains bound and the barrel points towards the only direction it can still answer.

Beyond the wall, the Sidi’s trenches have moved closer.

Behind it, Sillitoe begins another page of the dead.

Tanner places Hobbs’s quadrant beside the vent tools and sets his scaled feet against the carriage.

The Pelican in Wapping is six thousand miles away.

It is the first place left to which anything may still be carried.
