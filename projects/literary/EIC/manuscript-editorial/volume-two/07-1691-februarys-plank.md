# Part II: The Price of a Man

## Chapter 7: February's Plank

> **Wapping and Blackwall, February 1691**
>
> *“The plank did not fall. It turned.”*
>
> — Anders Blom

### I. The Sailing Tide

Elias leaves the Pelican before daylight with his tools upon his left shoulder.

His right hand will no longer close around the handle of the chest. The knuckles have risen into four pale hills beneath the skin and the middle finger points a little away from the others, as though it has begun disagreeing with the hand to which it belongs. He binds it in a strip of flannel, pulls his mitten over the swelling and says the cold will do what rest has failed to do.

February has put a skin upon every water butt in Wapping. The river stairs shine black beneath the frost. Rigging creaks above the Pool without wind, each frozen rope complaining softly as the tide turns the ships at anchor.

Esther stands inside the open door with one hand upon the bolt.

Bell's yard has other carpenters.

Bell's yard has other names in its book, Elias says.

Can the names lift a plank?

They can draw the pay.

Anders comes down the stairs carrying the Pelican's river shutter under one arm. He has removed it again because the lower hinge binds whenever frost lifts the sill. Without his own tools, which remain in a locked chest at Blackwall while three offices decide whether he earned them in foreign service, he has laid out Elias's upon a folded sack: plane, square, mallet, brace, three augers and a row of chisels darkened by years of sharpening.

He looks at Elias's hand.

You cannot feel the edge with that.

I can feel it when I strike it.

That is not the same.

It is to the man paying by the tide.

Elias takes the narrow mortising chisel from the sack. Its ash handle has split once and been bound with two turns of copper wire. He tests the edge against his left thumbnail, wraps it in oiled cloth and returns it to the chest.

Anders watches the lid close.

What are they lifting?

A lower wale. Green oak. Near thirty feet.

How many purchases?

Two, if Goffe has not sold one since yesterday.

And how many men?

Enough until somebody counts them.

He bends to the chest. Esther puts her boot against it.

Elias looks up.

If I miss the boat, I miss the day's hire.

Your hand is already missing it.

Then the other must earn double.

From the back room Tanner coughs. The sound begins deep, catches upon something and returns weaker. Elias turns his head towards it. A week earlier he would have remained to mend the cracked bed rail, fetch the broth or sit through the ague. This morning Bell's foreman has sent a boy twice before six with the same message: the Company's ship must take the evening tide from the dock, and every hour lost will be charged downwards until it reaches the men.

Esther removes her boot.

Anders lifts the other handle of the chest.

I am going to Blackwall, he says.

Your tools will not run away.

They have had practice.

They carry the chest between them into the dark.

Esther watches until the frost and river smoke take both men. Then she closes the door, lifts the bar into its brackets and writes two breakfasts upon the house slate.

She does not draw a line through either.

### II. The Lift

At Blackwall the outward-bound ship stands in the dry dock with her lower hull open to the morning.

The dock has been emptied upon the ebb until black mud shines between the floor timbers. Oak shores rise from it like the ribs of another vessel built around the first. Above them, the Indiaman's flank leans outward, tar-black and enormous, with one length of lower wale stripped away along the larboard side. The missing strake shows pale wood amid old pitch, a wound thirty feet long.

The new plank lies upon trestles beside it.

It is green oak, broad as Elias's chest and thicker than his hand is long. The yard has already worked heat and water into it until it accepts part of the hull's curve. Iron dogs bite its upper edge. Two hemp slings pass beneath it, each joined to a purchase hanging from the yard's lifting frame. The hauling falls lead towards a capstan above the dock.

There should be room enough to lift it slowly.

There is never room enough after a sailing date has been written.

Samuel Goffe stands upon the staging with a watchman's staff tucked beneath his arm and a folded note in his coat. He is Bell's foreman, a thick-necked joiner who has learned enough figures to fear them. He has not ordered the Company's ship to sail. The Company has not ordered him to lift this plank. Henry Johnson's yard has hired Bell for the repair, Bell has hired Goffe for the men, and Goffe has found Elias and nine others at the gate.

The order has travelled through four clean hands before reaching the swollen one.

Edmund Pelling stands above the dock in the same City shoes he wore at the Pelican. He has come to witness whether the vessel clears the dock upon the tide entered in the Company's sailing book. He carries no adze, commands no capstan and knows the weight of the plank only as a sum charged per foot.

Goffe waves the folded note at the men.

Miss this water and the owner loses two days. Bell pays the first penalty, I pay the second and you gentlemen may divide what remains.

One of the shore carpenters asks for a fresh sling.

Goffe points to the one beneath the after third of the plank.

That one held the floor timber.

It rubbed the whole way.

Then turn the rubbed part up.

Anders crouches beside it.

The outer yarns have been flattened upon one face until the tar shines. Where the sling returns around the lower arris of the oak, the worn place lies beneath the greatest bite of the load. A folded scrap of old sailcloth has been pushed between rope and timber, but half of it has slipped out.

Change this, Anders says.

Goffe looks down from the staging.

Are you on the gang?

I am looking for my tools.

Then look somewhere else.

Elias lowers himself beside Anders. He presses the sling with his left thumb. The rope does not spring back.

He looks upwards along the fall. The after purchase is not leading square to the plank. Its hauling part crosses towards the capstan at an angle and bears hard against one cheek of the leading block. The wood there has taken a bright new scar.

Shift the block, Elias says.

We shift it and lose an hour.

Then lose the hour.

Goffe steps down one rung.

Will your hand be fit tomorrow, Finch?

Elias says nothing.

Will it?

No.

Then perhaps you understand the value of this morning.

Goffe orders another piece of canvas pushed beneath the sling. He orders two men to the capstan bars and four to the hauling falls. Elias goes to the after end with a handspike under his left arm. Anders remains outside the gang until Goffe sees him looking and points at an empty capstan bar.

You want your chest examined today?

Yes.

Then make yourself useful where somebody can see it.

Pelling opens his attendance book.

The capstan begins to turn.

The men walk the bars round with their heads down and their shoulders against the wood. Pawls strike one iron notch after another. The sound passes through the frozen yard.

Clack. Breathe. Clack.

Both purchases tighten. The slings narrow against the green oak. The plank rises from its trestles by the width of a finger, settles, rises again.

At the after purchase the unfair fall grinds along the block cheek. Each movement draws the damaged place of the sling harder against the plank's edge.

Elias watches it.

Hold, he calls.

Goffe calls heave.

The forward end reaches the hull first. A carpenter sets a timber shore beneath it so the men may change their grip and bring the after end round. The weight divides itself among the forward sling, the shore and the chafed after sling. For a moment the lift becomes safer by accident.

Elias drives his handspike against a dog and levers the lower edge towards the empty strake. The plank moves an inch.

Again.

Another inch.

The curve is wrong at the after end. It stands away from the hull far enough to admit a fist.

Back rope, Elias says. Give me a line aft and we can check the swing.

After the shore is out, Goffe says.

Before.

The tide will not wait for your hand.

Nor will the plank.

Goffe looks towards Pelling.

The clerk does not nod. He does not need to. He looks at the yard clock and writes the time.

Goffe points at the shore.

Knock it clear.

The carpenter below hesitates.

It is still carrying.

Take the strain, then knock it clear.

Men lean into the capstan. The pawls quicken. The forward purchase tightens, but the after fall has reached the side of its block and will take no fairer line. The plank rises against the shore, not off it. The head remains hard beneath the oak.

Elias steps sideways, beyond the place where the oak would land if both slings let go. He puts the handspike against the after dog to keep the end from bearing into the staging.

Anders leaves the capstan bar.

Wait.

Goffe raises his staff.

Now.

The carpenter strikes the shore with a maul.

The first blow moves it half an inch.

The second drives its foot across the dock floor.

The shore kicks free.

All the weight it carried enters the two slings at once. The after purchase jerks sideways. The worn rope flattens against the oak until the surviving yarns are no thicker than twine.

They part with a crack like a pistol in a church.

The after end does not fall straight down.

The forward sling holds.

For one suspended instant it becomes a hinge. That sling lies close against the hull. The after end stands a fist out towards the dock wall and is already moving sideways under the crooked lead. Thirty feet of green oak turns beneath the one fixed point. The end sweeps across the dock faster than a man can step and lower than a man can duck.

Anders sees Elias beyond the falling place.

He also sees the wall behind him.

The plank takes Elias across the side and carries him into the granite.

The sound is not loud. It is the sound a full cask makes when a lighter touches the quay.

Then every loose thing moves.

The forward sling twists. Men drop the capstan bars. A handspike strikes the dock floor and leaps into the mud. The freed end of the parted rope whips across the staging. Goffe loses his staff and Pelling loses his book.

Anders reaches Elias before the plank settles.

Only the oak's after corner holds him now. Six men take bars to it. Two seize the surviving fall. Goffe shouts three orders, each cancelling the last, until the men stop listening and heave together.

They lift the corner the depth of a thumb.

Anders pulls Elias clear.

His body comes easily. Too easily. His left mitten remains beneath the plank with the handspike.

Elias is awake.

His eyes find Anders without finding the yard between them.

What moved? he asks.

The plank.

Did it fall?

No.

Elias considers this. His right hand opens once inside the flannel binding, all four swollen fingers loosening as though something has finally released them.

Then he stops breathing.

Above the dock Pelling retrieves his book from the frost.

The page has landed open at the sailing tide.

### III. What Comes Home

Four yard men bring Elias back to the Pelican upon a staging plank.

They arrive by the river door shortly after noon. Somebody has put a folded sailcloth beneath his head and crossed his hands upon his coat. The right mitten is missing. Frost has melted in his hair.

Esther looks first at his face, then at the plank, then at the men carrying it.

Where is Anders?

Still at the yard.

Where are the tools?

Coming after.

Who says?

Mr Goffe.

Then Mr Goffe may remain where he is until they do.

She moves two tables together in the public room. The men set the staging plank across them. One asks whether she wants the body taken upstairs.

No.

There are customers due at dinner.

They may dine elsewhere.

The men stand with their caps in their hands. They have carried shipmates, brothers and strangers through doors like this, but the woman before them offers no weeping by which they may know when to leave.

One of them clears his throat.

He was not beneath it, mistress.

Esther removes the folded cloth from under Elias's head. It belongs to the yard and bears a black inventory number in one corner.

I know where he is.

I mean he did not stand foolish.

What is your name?

Matthew Pike.

She takes the chalk from beside the beer slate and writes it upon the back of Pelling's copied casualty sheet.

You saw where he stood?

I did.

Come back after your shift.

The man looks towards Elias.

What for?

To say it while he is still here.

She bolts the river door behind them.

Tanner comes down in his stockings, one hand upon the wall. Fever has left him weak and angry with weakness. He stops at the last stair.

The scaled boots remain beside his bed.

Who?

Esther lifts Elias's left hand and removes the mitten. There is black oak dust in the cuff.

Bell's yard.

Tanner looks at the body, at the plank beneath it and at the two tables made level with pieces of folded card.

Company ship?

Company tide.

He sits upon the stair because there is nowhere else in him to go.

Esther brings the basin used for rinsing mugs, empties it, scalds it and fills it again. She washes the frost, mud and dock grit from Elias's face. She does not cover him. Not yet.

The house has received a body without a cause, tools without an inventory and a debt without an owner.

She means to keep all three visible.

### IV. What Failed

Anders returns at three carrying Elias's chest in one hand and a coil of severed rope in the other.

There is blood upon the shoulder of his coat. None of it is his. He places the chest beneath the bar and lays the rope upon the floor where the winter light from the river window can reach it.

Esther looks at the rope.

Did that kill him?

No.

He points to the staging plank beneath Elias.

That did.

Did it fall?

The plank did not fall. It turned.

He takes the poker from the hearth and balances it across a pewter mug. One end rests upon the floor. He loops a piece of bottle cord around the other and holds both ends as if they are the two slings.

This is the plank. This mug is the forward sling. The floor is the shore.

He lifts the end from the floor.

When they struck the shore, its part of the weight came into the ropes. This one was already half a rope.

He puts down the poker and opens the parted sling across the boards.

The break is not uniform. Most of the hemp has gone dark, flat and polished for the length of a palm where it worked against the oak. Only a small heart of pale fibres has torn cleanly at the moment of failure.

Anders separates them with Elias's awl.

This is old, he says, touching the blackened outer yarns. This is this morning.

Tanner has crossed the room in his stockings. He lowers himself beside the rope.

Why did the other sling not hold it?

It did.

Anders balances the poker upon the mug again. He lets the unsupported end turn through a slow arc until it touches the leg of the table.

That is why Elias is dead.

He shows them the shining rub upon one side of the fall and draws the route of the hauling part with his finger.

The lead was crooked. It pulled the end towards the wall. The shore carried that end until Goffe ordered it struck. When the worn sling parted, the sound sling held and made a hinge. A back rope would have checked the swing.

Was there one?

There is one in Goffe's inventory.

Was there one on the plank?

No.

Esther looks at Elias.

Can you prove this?

Anders touches the dark chafe, the pale break and the hard shine left by the block cheek.

It is proved.

Can you prove it to paper?

His hand stops.

The rope is from Bell's store. The block belongs to Johnson's yard. Goffe gave the order. The ship belongs to her owners. The tide belongs to the Company.

And Elias?

Bell paid him by the day.

The answer moves through the room and finds no place to settle.

Esther coils the broken sling neatly, lays it beneath the table supporting Elias and puts Goffe's inventory on top of it.

Then paper may explain which one buried him.

### V. Whose Man

Pelling arrives before dark with a black-edged notice and Elias's tool list.

He pauses when he sees the public room. Elias remains upon the staging plank between the tables. Two candles burn at his head because Esther has decided the yard's numbered cloth will not touch him again.

Pelling removes his hat.

I am sorry, Mrs Finch.

For which part?

He looks at the rope beneath the table.

I was present.

Then you may begin there.

He reads the notice because she does not take it.

It records that Elias Finch, shipwright, suffered a mortal hurt while employed upon repairs to an outward-bound vessel at Blackwall. It expresses the owner's regret. It states that the East India Company exercised no direction over the methods or persons used in the repair. The yard's account lies against Bell. Elias's hire lies against Goffe. Any claim for wages, physic or burial must therefore be addressed to the contractor by whom he was immediately retained.

When Pelling finishes, Tanner speaks from the settle.

Who required the tide?

The Company's sailing office entered it.

Who charged the owner for missing it?

That is a charter provision.

Who charged Bell?

The yard, I suppose.

Who hurried Goffe?

Pelling folds the notice along its existing crease.

No person admits to hurrying him.

Tanner looks at Elias.

Then haste came to the yard alone.

Pelling's face reddens. He is young enough to feel the shape of the trap and trained enough to remain inside it.

The Company did not employ Mr Finch.

It examined his witnesses here last week, Esther says.

That was a separate matter.

It offered Tanner work here.

Also separate.

It set today's tide.

Separate.

It has made a powerful number of separate things out of one dead man.

Pelling puts the notice upon the bar. He does not ask her to sign it.

There may be a charitable allowance towards burial if poverty is certified by the parish.

Elias owned tools.

Then poverty may be disputed.

He owned the things by which he prevented poverty.

I do not make the rule.

No, Esther says. You bring it indoors.

Pelling looks again at the body. At Blackwall he watched the men raise the oak and saw Elias drawn from beneath its corner. The Company's sailing book remained in his hands throughout. He has spent the afternoon learning that an object may be present at a death without accepting responsibility for it.

I can record Anders Blom's account, he says.

Will it alter the notice?

No.

Will it pay for the burial?

No.

Will it stop Goffe doing the same tomorrow?

Pelling looks at the parted sling.

It might require the rope to be condemned.

Esther places Goffe's inventory beside his clean sheet.

Then begin with the rope and do not stop writing when it becomes expensive.

### VI. Nine Chisels

They open Elias's chest upon the bar.

Esther reads Goffe's inventory. Anders lifts each tool into the candlelight and Tanner makes a second list because Pelling's hand is occupied with the failed tackle.

One adze.

One.

One hand axe.

One.

Two planes, working.

Two.

One brace with six bits.

One and six.

Three augers.

Three.

One square, one bevel, one chalk reel, one saw, one mallet.

Each appears. The square bears the bright place made by Elias's thumb. The chalk line is still blue from the Pelican shutter. A tooth near the heel of the saw has been set too far to port because his swollen hand slipped upon it two nights earlier.

Pelling reads the final line.

Nine chisels assorted.

Anders places them upon the bar from broadest to narrowest.

Eight.

Pelling checks the chest corners and the tray.

The yard count says nine.

Elias left here with nine, Esther says.

You saw them?

I watched him wrap the narrow one.

It may remain at the work.

Then the work may return it.

Pelling looks at the rain striking the window.

The yard gates close at five.

So does this house.

I have delivered the listed effects.

You have delivered eight chisels against a list of nine.

I cannot be responsible for a contractor's tool chest.

Esther turns the receipt towards him. Beneath the printed words *delivered complete* she draws one slow line from margin to margin.

Then you cannot be responsible for my mark.

Pelling stands before the bar with his hat in his hands. Nothing in his instruction requires him to recover a dead carpenter's chisel. Nothing allows him to close the day's attendance without a receipt for the effects transferred in his presence.

Esther waits.

He puts on his wet coat.

Describe it.

Narrow mortising chisel, Anders says. Ash handle. Split at the head. Copper wire twice round.

Pelling writes the description upon his cuff paper.

When he leaves, Esther bars the door behind him.

Matthew Pike returns after his shift and stands beside Elias. He describes the lift without knowing Anders has already done so. He places Elias beyond the drop, Goffe upon the staging and the missing back rope coiled beside the capstan where nobody had bent it on. Tanner writes his words beneath his name.

Two other yard men come before seven.

One has seen the old chafe. The other struck the shore.

I thought they had it, he says.

Who?

The ropes.

Esther gives him beer without charging it.

Pelling returns at half past eight, soaked through and carrying the narrow chisel inside his coat.

They found it beneath the after staging beside Elias's mitten. The copper wire has taken a fresh dent where the plank crossed it. A little green oak remains upon the edge.

He lays it beside the other eight.

Nine, Esther says.

Nine, Pelling agrees.

She signs only for the tools. She does not sign the notice of no liability.

Pelling leaves his copy of Anders's account beneath the bar. He has marked the sling *to be withdrawn from service* and the missing preventer *not produced upon inspection*. Neither sentence admits who killed Elias. Both make tomorrow's lie slightly harder to write.

After he goes, Esther takes the narrow chisel upstairs to the roof hatch.

The wooden pin has bound since the great wind. Elias found the fault but his hand became too swollen to cut a new mortise. Anders has shaped a replacement from oak and left it unfinished upon the landing.

She gives him the chisel.

This is Elias's, he says.

Yes.

What do you want me to do with it?

The work.

Anders kneels beneath the hatch. He sets the edge into the timber and raises Elias's mallet.

Below them, Tanner keeps watch beside the body. Three statements, a severed sling and the unsigned notice lie beneath the Pelican's bar. The Company ship waits for another tide. Somewhere at Blackwall another carpenter will be offered the place Elias has left.

The first mallet stroke travels through the house.

Then the second.

Esther stands upon the stair and listens until the new pin fits.
