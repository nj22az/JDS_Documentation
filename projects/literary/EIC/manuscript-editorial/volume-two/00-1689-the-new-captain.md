# Prologue: The New Captain

> **Nevis, August 1689**
>
> *“The men who make a captain may make another.”*
>
> — Jonah Fletcher

## I. The Ship with Two Names

The ship is French on Monday and English by Thursday, which is quick work even for the Caribbean.

She lies in the road off Charlestown with sixteen guns, a damaged foretopsail and a new name drying across her stern. The old letters have been planed away in the heat. *BLESSED WILLIAM* shows pale against the darker wood, the painter having charged extra for gold and received an answer involving a bucket.

Nobody has settled which William is blessed. The new King in London has the better claim and the poorer acquaintance with the vessel. William Kidd, newly chosen to command her, does not discourage the other interpretation.

He has been captain for three days.

The change shows first in the way he comes ashore. As a seaman he would have stepped from the boat with the painter in one hand and his shoes in the other, because surf does not improve leather and no title makes a wet landing dry. As captain he waits for two men to steady the boat, misjudges the retreat of the water, and fills his right shoe before reaching the sand.

Robert Culliford sees it and looks away too late.

Not a word, Kidd says.

I said nothing.

You arranged your face.

It arranges itself.

Kidd takes off the shoe, pours Nevis out of it, and puts it back on. The men behind him watch with the blankness of a crew learning how much humour its new master will bear. He bears this much.

The packet inside his coat has stayed dry. He touches it once through the cloth as they cross the strip between the landing place and the warehouses. It contains a direction under Governor Codrington’s authority that places the *Blessed William* at the service of the island, recognises Kidd as her captain for that service and permits the taking of French vessels and goods in time of war.

The paper does not promise wages.

Codrington explained that point with the ease of a man standing inside a house built of stone. Nevis requires defence. A capable crew will find its reward among the French islands. Any lawful prize, once condemned, will pay captain, men and owners according to their interests.

Kidd asked what the government’s interest would be.

The governor’s secretary answered that the necessary schedule would be supplied.

The warehouses along the shore are full of necessity already. Sugar comes out in hogsheads. Salt beef, powder, hoop iron and English cloth go in. Men hired from the estates roll the casks under an overseer who calls each by a name not one of them uses for himself. The figures are entered by a clerk in a shaded doorway. The clerk counts forty-three hogsheads and no men.

Kidd slows to let a cart pass.

There, Culliford says, is an island unable to pay its defenders.

It pays the people who own it.

That may explain why they own it.

Kidd looks towards him.

Culliford served beside him before the mutiny and voted for him after it. He is lean, Cornish, several years younger and pleased by any weakness in a thing expected to govern him. The vote is the fact Kidd dislikes remembering when Culliford uses that tone. He has not inherited the *Blessed William*. He has been raised to her quarterdeck by men who can still recall the sound of his name without *captain* before it.

The Blue Lantern stands behind a chandlery near the water. It has a public room in front, a smaller room behind, and shutters made from timber that has previously failed at some other employment. Kidd has told the crew to meet there at sunset. He intends to read the governor’s direction aloud, settle the division of future prizes and return aboard with every man understanding that three days of uncertainty have ended.

From inside comes the scrape of a fiddle attempting the same phrase until either the player or the tune surrenders.

Culliford puts a hand on the door.

Who is Fletcher? Kidd asks.

The hand stops.

You invited him.

I said the bald old man from the careening beach. There were three.

Only one made the other two move.

Why?

Culliford considers the question.

Because they knew who he was.

He opens the door.

## II. The Direction

Jonah Fletcher sits in the back room where the air moves least and watches the doorway through the reflection in a dark window.

By his own reckoning he is sixty-four years old. A Dutch muster at Curaçao once made him sixty-one; a Barbados warrant made him seventy-two. Jonah accepts whichever age inconveniences the man asking.

Time has stripped his scalp clean without thinning the beard beneath it. The beard is still black around the mouth and iron-grey towards the ends. His left ear has been split, his nose broken more than once, and two fingers of his right hand set crooked after healing without a surgeon. A white, puckered shot-mark shows above his collar when he bends. None of it makes him appear fragile.

He sits with both feet planted and his coat buttoned despite the heat. A cut length of hemp lies across his knees. He is putting a whipping on the end with black waxed twine, taking each turn close against the last. Beside his right hand rests a lead musket ball flattened on one side.

The room has arranged itself around him.

Lounging men have found other walls. A bench that ordinarily holds three holds two. Nobody has been instructed to move. Jonah possesses the sort of authority that works best before it becomes speech.

Kidd has seen officers attempt the effect with uniforms, pistols, money and public cruelty. This is not the same. Jonah looks at a man as if the useful and useless parts have already separated themselves and are awaiting his decision.

The harbour gives Fletcher a different history according to who is frightened of him. He has been a gunner in a Dutch squadron, a master’s mate under an English commission, a prisoner at Cartagena and the only sober man in a Curaçao mutiny. The accounts disagree about years, flags and bodies. They agree that when Jonah Fletcher tells a man to put down a knife, the knife generally reaches the table before the question.

Kidd dislikes him immediately.

Fletcher.

Jonah finishes the turn he is making. He draws the twine tight with his teeth, cuts it and presses the end beneath his thumb.

Captain, he says.

The word is correct. Nothing in the way he says it acknowledges a change in the world.

Kidd removes the packet from his coat and lays it on the table.

We have the governor’s authority.

Jonah places the flattened ball on one corner before the turning air can lift it. His thumbnail is split from an old injury and has grown back in two ridges.

Authority to do what?

Kidd looks towards the room. Eleven of his men have come. The others are keeping the ship or spending advances they have not received. A boy from the chandlery brings a jug and cups. The keeper follows him and holds out her hand.

Your account, Captain.

Put it to the ship.

She keeps her hand out.

The ship has not drunk it.

Culliford looks at the ceiling. Somebody at the bench coughs into his fist.

Kidd pays. The keeper bites the coin, gives it to the boy and leaves the door open behind her.

The jug has achieved what the governor’s seal has not. Every man attends.

Kidd breaks the wafer, unfolds the direction and begins to read. He has a good voice for a deck: clear without strain and carrying further than anger. The document names their sovereigns, the war, the danger to the Leeward Islands, the governor and council, the vessel lately brought into English possession and the person of Captain William Kidd. The crew hears that portion with suitable gravity.

It directs Kidd to join such forces as the governor appoints, resist the French enemy, preserve English shipping and pursue lawful opportunities against vessels, goods and settlements belonging to the French Crown or its subjects. Such captures are to be brought before proper authority and divided after adjudication, deductions and fees.

The paper grows less popular as it continues.

Kidd reaches the final sentence and folds it.

We sail with protection of the government, he says. What we take lawfully will be condemned lawfully. Every man receives a share.

After deductions, says Culliford.

The court has its fee.

The governor?

His schedule is to come.

An older gunner named Mace leans against the wall.

What is our pay for keeping his island?

Our pay is what we take.

And if the French stay home?

They have not stayed home in twenty years.

That is true and therefore insufficient. The men drink.

Jonah has not touched his cup. He turns the folded direction so that its edge lies square with the grain of the table.

Read the part where the governor pays for powder, he says.

The room goes still enough for the fiddle in front to be wrong by itself.

Stores will be accounted after service, Kidd says.

Read it.

The direction does not list every arrangement.

Then read the arrangement that does.

Kidd puts one hand flat on the paper.

You were invited to hear the terms, Fletcher.

I heard them.

Then you know the governor has placed us in lawful service.

Jonah looks at Kidd’s hand rather than his face.

He has placed you where you may earn money without his spending any.

Mace gives a short sound and converts it into a cough.

Kidd feels the room alter. It is not opposition yet. It is worse: calculation. Eleven men have begun valuing the governor’s seal against food, powder and time.

Codrington requires ships now, Kidd says. He will remember those who answer.

Jonah lifts his gaze.

Will he?

He does not make the question large. That makes it difficult to strike.

## III. The Men Who Made Him

Kidd expected an old sailor who could be displayed to the crew like a sound piece of timber: proof that experienced men approve the venture. He did not expect the timber to express an opinion about the builder.

How long since you commanded a ship? he asks.

Never did.

The answer takes some of the force from Kidd’s next sentence.

Then perhaps you do not know what a captain must settle.

Jonah rests both hands on the table.

I know what he cannot.

Which is?

The price of an empty sea.

Culliford examines his cup. Mace looks directly at Jonah. The younger hands look between them, discovering that a dispute between two men is often a safer place to stand than loyalty to either.

Kidd sits opposite.

The French hold St Christopher, Martinique, Guadeloupe and half the water between. Their merchants cannot keep every hull in harbour. Their warships cannot defend every merchant. We have a fast vessel, sixteen guns and men who have already taken her once.

From a captain who trusted them, Jonah says.

Nobody moves.

Kidd’s hand closes.

The French captain would have sold the English aboard him the moment war reached his ear.

Likely.

We acted first.

Aye.

You condemn it?

No.

Jonah’s calm leaves Kidd nowhere to put the defence he has prepared.

Then what is your complaint?

You speak of taking the ship as proof the men will follow you. It proves they know how to take a ship.

The room contracts around the table.

Culliford’s face has lost its amusement. Mace sets down his cup. Two men near the door turn towards the exit.

Kidd sees it all and sees that Jonah has seen it first. He understands why the room makes space. A look towards Culliford, a question to Mace, one sentence about shares, and the vote that raised William Kidd three days ago might be recalled.

For the first time since coming ashore, Kidd is afraid.

It is not fear that Jonah will strike him. Half the room carries knives and Kidd knows what to do with those. It is the recognition that violence will not restore his authority.

Jonah lets the silence stand until everybody understands it. Then he takes the lead ball from the paper and rolls it beneath his palm.

Who made you captain? he asks.

The crew elected me.

Who may make another?

Kidd does not answer.

Jonah looks at the men.

Nobody volunteers. That is an answer of its own.

The men who make a captain may make another, Jonah says. The paper changes none of it. It only gives them something to blame you for when the shares are poor.

Culliford leans forward.

And what would you have us do? Refuse the governor? Give the ship back to France? Take honest employment cutting cane?

The last suggestion draws no laughter. Outside, a cart passes towards the quay under a load of sugar. The crack of an overseer’s whip travels further than the cart wheels.

Jonah looks towards the sound.

The island is rich, he says. The men who made it rich are not. That should tell you how often labour and payment meet merely because somebody wrote an account.

He turns back to Kidd.

Write the shares before you sail. Write who pays powder. Write who owns a prize while the court considers whether it was French on the correct day. Write what a wounded man receives if there is no prize.

The governor’s direction governs the service.

The governor’s direction governs the governor.

Kidd feels Culliford’s attention sharpen. The old man is not merely undermining him now. He is offering terms around which the crew can organise.

You speak boldly for a man not aboard, Kidd says.

Jonah’s good eye settles on him.

You called me old when you came in. Say that instead.

Kidd has not called him old. He thought it, and something in his face allowed the thought out.

Jonah waits.

No one in the room reaches for a drink.

Kidd has stood before armed men in a taken ship and convinced them that his command was safer than the captain’s. He watched the French officers put ashore and did not move when one promised to see him broken. He knows the practical use of appearing unafraid.

He also knows when appearance has become expensive.

You are old, he says.

Aye.

Older than any sailor I have met.

That is not command. It is persistence.

And you think persistence gives you leave to question mine?

No. Your men give me leave. They invited the question when they listened.

Jonah looks away first, but it does not feel like victory. He unfolds the direction again.

## IV. What Counts as French

Jonah reads slowly. His finger moves beneath the lines, stopping at the turns of the secretary’s hand. Kidd has assumed he cannot read. He is annoyed to discover that the assumption belongs to him rather than Jonah.

The old man reaches the phrase *belonging to the French King or any of his subjects*.

What belongs? he asks.

The vessels and goods.

How do you know?

By their papers.

Papers may lie.

By the flag.

Flags lie sooner.

By the master’s declaration, the port of origin, the ownership and the cargo. By evidence, Fletcher. The same way any prize court decides.

Jonah nods.

A Bristol hull leaves Martinique under French colours. Her master is Irish. Half the sugar belongs to a Dutch factor and the other half to an English planter who used a French name to avoid a duty. French?

If she sails under French protection in time of war, she is liable.

The English planter will say otherwise.

He may say it to the court.

After you take his ship.

Yes.

After your men open the cargo.

They will not open it without order.

Somebody near the wall shifts.

After the sugar is sold here because you cannot feed a prize crew all season, Jonah continues. After the governor takes his fee. After the Dutch factor writes to Curaçao, the Irish master swears you knew him for a friend and the English owner discovers a cousin at Whitehall. What are you then?

Acting under this.

Kidd taps the direction.

Jonah puts the ball on it again.

That is what you are today.

The words are quiet. Kidd hears something close behind them—not prophecy, which belongs to priests and cheats, but experience repeated until it no longer needs imagination.

You think no commission is safe.

I think safety is not what it sells.

Then what?

Permission.

For lawful action.

Permission for action another man wishes taken and does not wish attached to his own hand.

Kidd sits back.

You would have every captain refuse public service because the public may later prove ungrateful.

No.

You would have us starve until a perfect document is written.

No.

Then give me an order instead of a warning.

Jonah considers him for a long moment.

Make the paper true while you have the power.

How?

Write the men’s bargain. Keep account before the gold comes aboard. Do not let the governor’s clerk keep the only copy. When you take a vessel, learn whose loss you are making before you call it French. If you must do a wrong thing, do not use the seal to lie to yourself about it.

That last sentence reaches further into Kidd than the others.

Kidd wants the paper to divide the sea into what might and might not be taken, and command to divide the deck into decision and obedience. Jonah has made both uncertain without asking him to surrender either.

Kidd dislikes him more carefully now.

Mace pours the old man a fresh measure.

Jonah covers the cup with two fingers.

You do not drink? Kidd asks.

I have.

The answer ends the subject.

Rain strikes the shutters. It arrives without a beginning, hard enough that conversation in the front room stops beneath it. Wind pushes smoke down the chimney and sends the flame of the wall lamp flat.

The boy from the chandlery runs through the open door.

Boat’s against the quay, he says. Line’s gone.

Every seaman in the room moves.

## V. The Line

The ship’s boat has come in with four more hands and two small casks of water. Her bow has been made fast to an iron ring in the quay wall and her stern to a timber pile. The sternfast parts in the first gust. Wind and the run along the stones swing her outward until the bowline takes the whole strain.

One of the men tries to snub the line around a post.

The turn crosses over his wrist.

He is nineteen, perhaps twenty, a broad Devon hand called Hugh Dyer who voted for Kidd because Kidd remembers the names of ordinary men. He stands with his shoulder against the post and his trapped hand disappearing beneath two tightening parts of wet hemp. Each movement of the boat draws the line closer to bone.

Three men haul at the free end.

Stop hauling, Jonah says.

They do not hear him through the rain.

Jonah steps into the space between them and Dyer. He does not shout. He looks once at the nearest man and points down.

Hands come off the line.

The boat falls back, takes the strain and drives Dyer’s wrist harder against the post. He makes a sound he will later deny.

Knife, Jonah says.

Kidd is at the tavern door with the governor’s direction inside his coat. When the rain strikes the table, he folds it once, puts it away and then follows the men out.

He sees the line, the boat, the trapped wrist and Jonah’s open hand.

Cutting the bowline will save Dyer’s hand and release the boat into the road. Four men are aboard her, one with an oar, none ready at the sails. Downwind lie anchored hulls, stone and the open passage beyond the point. A captain’s boat is not easily replaced. A seaman’s hand is not replaced at all.

Kidd draws his knife.

Culliford catches his sleeve.

Slack it from the ring.

The knot has drawn until its turns appear carved.

It will not slack.

Use the cask to check her.

No time.

The line tightens by another fraction. Dyer’s face empties.

Kidd puts the blade against the hemp.

Jonah takes his wrist.

The grip is dry, hard and astonishingly strong.

Not there, he says.

He points six feet nearer the boat, beyond the crossed turn but before the lead into the water.

Cut close and the end will run through his hand.

Kidd moves. He sees it then: cut beside the post and the released weight will pull the severed end through the very crossing that holds Dyer. Wet hemp under load can open flesh as neatly as iron.

He steps over the line, reaches beyond the trapped wrist and sets the knife where Jonah has shown him.

Ready aft! he calls.

The men in the boat understand enough to get low. One ships the oar against the quay. Another seizes the water cask lashing.

Kidd cuts.

The rope parts one strand at a time, each giving with a sound lost beneath the rain. When the last strand goes, the boat swings free. The severed end whips across the stones and vanishes over the edge.

Dyer falls against the post with his hand held to his chest.

The boat drives outward. Her single oar strikes stone and holds. Men on the quay catch a thrown line on the second attempt. The boat stops broadside to the rain, half full of curses and still afloat.

Jonah kneels beside Dyer.

The wrist is crushed and the skin stripped, but the hand remains attached. Jonah touches each finger. Dyer swears at the first, feels nothing at the second, and tries to strike him with the other hand at the third.

Good, Jonah says.

What is?

You chose the correct hand.

He takes two straight pieces from a broken crate and binds the wrist with the black twine he carried from the tavern. His bent fingers work without haste. The men stand around him in the rain until he looks up.

Surgeon.

Two move.

Not the estate butcher. The Dutchman by the church.

They lift Dyer.

Who pays him? Culliford asks.

The question is not mockery now.

Kidd wipes his knife on his coat.

The ship.

From which account?

Mine until there is an account.

Mace watches him.

And the line?

Mine.

The boat?

If she is damaged, mine.

Jonah rises. Water runs from his beard and the end of his nose. He looks diminished by rain until he meets Kidd’s eyes, and then age becomes irrelevant again.

You saved the paper, he says.

Kidd’s hand goes to his coat.

Before you came out.

It would have been ruined.

There are clerks.

There is one Dyer.

I cut the line.

After you saved the paper.

Kidd feels the men listening behind him.

It was an instant.

Aye.

Any man might have done the same.

Aye.

Jonah gives him nothing to oppose. The agreement is worse than accusation.

Kidd steps closer.

Do you imagine I value a seal above a man’s hand?

I do not have to imagine it. I watched which one you reached for first.

The rain eases. It moves east across the roadstead in a grey wall, exposing the *Blessed William* beyond it. Her new name shines wetly at the stern.

Kidd watches Dyer being carried up the street. He has not known the boy long. That is no defence offered by command.

Jonah retrieves the cut end of the line from beside the post. He opens the strands where Kidd’s knife has passed and examines them.

Rot in the heart, he says.

The outside looks sound.

Most things arrange that.

Kidd waits for another lesson. Jonah coils the ruined length and hands it to the chandlery boy.

Put it on the captain’s account.

The boy looks at Kidd.

Put it there, Kidd says.

## VI. Two Papers

They return to the back room wet enough to offend the furniture.

The keeper charges for cloths, a broken cup and the water that entered through a shutter she claims one of the crew opened. Nobody disputes her arithmetic. The governor’s direction lies safe inside Kidd’s coat, dry except for a thumbprint at the fold.

Kidd asks for ink.

The keeper brings it after he pays.

He turns the spoiled side of a shipping notice upward and begins writing the agreement Jonah demanded. The first sentences are too formal. He strikes them through. The next leave all discretion with the captain. Culliford reads over his shoulder and makes a sound. Kidd strikes those through as well.

They settle shares before midnight.

Captain, officers, ordinary hands and boys each receive a stated part after defined deductions. Powder and provisions used in the governor’s service are to be entered separately. A wounded man will receive something even if no prize is taken. Hugh Dyer’s name becomes the first entry beneath the rule.

They argue over desertion, damage, private plunder, prisoners and captured holds. They do not solve every question. They make denial harder.

Mace and Culliford sign as witnesses. The other men make names or marks according to their ability. Kidd copies the agreement himself, once for the ship and once for the crew. The keeper supplies the second sheet at a rate that suggests she has anticipated the need.

Jonah watches from the end of the table.

When the last sand has been shaken from the copies, Kidd pushes one towards him.

Sign.

I am not aboard.

Witness it.

I saw men bargain. They do not need me to prove it.

They listen to you.

Tonight.

They would follow you.

No.

The answer comes without pride.

Why not?

Because I would not take them where they wish to go.

Kidd looks at the old man’s hands. The twine has left black wax in the split thumbnail. A small crescent of Dyer’s blood remains beside it.

How many seas have you crossed?

Jonah considers.

Enough to stop counting crossings.

How old are you?

Enough to stop helping other men count.

Kidd almost smiles. Jonah does not, but something in his face acknowledges the attempt.

You knew the line would run through his wrist.

I have seen it take a hand.

Yours?

No.

Whose?

Jonah picks up the flattened musket ball.

Kidd understands that the interview is over.

He folds the crew’s copy and gives it to Mace. His own goes inside his coat beside the governor’s direction. The two papers make a thickness he can feel when he buttons the cloth.

At the door Jonah speaks.

Captain.

This time the title carries weight. Kidd turns.

When this service profits, the governor will remember that he authorised it. When it shames him, he will remember what he did not write. Keep account of both.

I will.

Do not say it quickly.

Kidd waits.

I will keep account.

Jonah looks at the pocket holding the papers.

Keep those dry and see what they call you tomorrow.

The front room has emptied. Outside, water drops from the eaves into the flooded ruts of the lane. The air after rain is cooler by a degree and smells of wet earth beneath the sugar.

Kidd walks down towards the landing with Culliford, Mace and the crew behind him. Nobody laughs when he steps into the boat. Nobody steadies it for him either. He takes the painter himself, finds the right moment between the small waves and boards with dry shoes.

The men follow.

From the door of the Blue Lantern, Jonah watches the boat pull towards the ship with two names and a captain with two papers.

William Kidd believes he has learned to distrust one of them.
