# The Venture

*1603–1635*

A queen signs a paper on the last day of a dying century, and a company begins with a monopoly, a fleet and no idea what it will become. Three years later, on a wet night in Wapping, Tom Fletcher is preparing to sign away years of his life when Silas Rook comes through the Pelican door with a blood-covered woman in his grip and a simple story about a murdered clerk. Several people believe him. Then ordinary things begin not to fit: the weather, the mud, the timber dust, the timing, the way a working man holds a knife. Nobody forms a court and nobody delivers a verdict. People simply stop helping Rook. He escapes while attention turns to Matthew Bell, who is still alive in the breaking shed and dies after they bring him in.

Tom signs anyway.

Bell's dangerous page is kept dry beneath Maggie's bar because somebody has to keep it somewhere Harcourt's men will not immediately search. Over the next thirty-two years other papers and small objects join it for equally practical reasons. Tom becomes exceptionally useful inside the systems that first trapped his family. Maria Mori learns languages, paper and trade until she can set her own terms. Daniel Vale survives Amboyna with an account nobody knows how to use honestly. Anne Bell, Jack Mercer, Arthur Higgins and the people around the Pelican live forward from the same night without ever turning it into a founding myth.

The emotional spine is Tom and Maggie: the boy who leaves, the man who comes home believing usefulness has made his compromises clean, the fourteen years in which neither can cross the distance between them, and the final return after Maggie is gone. The wider history enters through work, debt, ships, wages, charters, records and the consequences people carry home.

Knowing what happened is not the same as being able to put it right. *The Venture* begins there.
