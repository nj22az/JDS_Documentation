# The Venture — Amboyna / 1626 Historical Check

Status: research control for the natural-opening revision. Checked 2026-08-08.

## Execution count and method

Modern scholarship reconstructs the 1623 Amboyna conspiracy trial as ending in the execution of:

- ten English East India Company men, including Gabriel Towerson;
- ten Japanese men associated with the VOC garrison;
- one Portuguese man.

The condemned were beheaded. Towerson's head was subsequently displayed.

The present Book One wording that has the Japanese men burned should therefore be removed.

Source: D. K. Bassett, `The “Amboyna Massacre” of 1623`, Cambridge; Alison Games, `Inventing the English Massacre`.

## Torture

Water torture using a cloth and repeated pouring/suffocation was central to the controversy and is well documented. Later English publications also alleged additional torture methods; Dutch accounts disputed some of the English descriptions.

Editorial rule: Daniel may directly describe the water torture he personally experienced. The narrator should not authenticate every later English propaganda detail.

## Daniel Vale's fictional survival

Historical records include accused men who were pardoned/acquitted rather than executed. Daniel remains fictional and can plausibly be a retained/transferred factor who survives the trial without changing the documented twenty-one executions.

His uncertainty over why he survived is therefore usable, but the novel should not imply he was literally the only survivor.

## English East India Company response

The existing 1626 chapter makes the EIC chiefly interested in suppressing Daniel's account and buying his silence.

This is historically weak.

The EIC did the opposite at institutional level: Amboyna became a major Anglo-Dutch grievance, and Company-backed/public English accounts emphasised Dutch cruelty and English innocence. Printed texts and images helped make the event into the durable English idea of the `Amboyna Massacre`.

Editorial correction:

- the Company should want Daniel to speak;
- it should want a **usable version** of his suffering;
- its representative may pressure Daniel to sign a prepared narrative, deposition or publication that simplifies nationalities, uncertainty and local complexity into an anti-Dutch English grievance;
- Daniel's resistance is therefore not `I refuse to be silenced`; it is `I refuse to let you own what happened because you can use it`;
- this is more consistent with the novel's theme that institutions turn grief into usable material.

Sources:
- Alison Games, `Inventing the English Massacre: Amboyna in History and Memory`, Oxford University Press, 2020.
- Oxford chapter `The Amboyna Business`.

## Nationality guardrail

Do not let Carter pouring out genever become the novel's moral verdict on Dutch people.

If retained, the gesture must be shown as emotionally understandable and intellectually wrong. Hendricks is a Dutch river-man who had nothing to do with Amboyna. His reaction should expose the difference between grief and collective blame.

## Historical ambiguity guardrail

The novel may let Daniel believe there was no conspiracy. It should not have the omniscient narrator declare every disputed question settled. Modern scholarship treats the surviving evidence and the later English/Dutch narratives critically.

## Revised 1626 dramatic purpose

The chapter should move:

Daniel survives a violent trial -> Tom and Maria Mori get him home -> Maggie first treats him as a person -> Daniel gives fragments of his own experience -> London/EIC wants the experience as national/company material -> Daniel sees his own trauma being edited for use -> he creates/keeps a version he can stand behind -> that true account enters the Pelican fault beside Bell's page -> Tom and Maggie reconcile through action rather than argument.
