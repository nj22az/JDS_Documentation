# Canon Decision — Maria Mori

Status: **APPROVED for the Book One redevelopment branch**

## Name

**Maria Mori** replaces **Maria de Sousa** throughout *The Venture*.

- **Maria** is her Christian/baptismal given name and is a name she consciously keeps.
- **Mori** is her Japanese birth-family surname.
- **de Sousa is deprecated legacy material** and must not be reintroduced into revised prose, character bibles, appendices, metadata, or deployed reader text.

## Character direction

Maria Mori is inspired in broad character conception by Lady Mariko of *Shōgun*: restraint, intelligence, aristocratic bearing, Christian identity, linguistic ability, disciplined observation, and controlled defiance. She is not to reproduce Mariko's biography, family, dialogue, or plot.

Maria's Japanese identity is fundamental, not a late reveal that changes who she was. Her European Christian given name and Japanese family surname allow her name to inhabit both worlds without giving her a Portuguese family identity.

The surname **Mori** is not intended to establish kinship with any historical Mōri clan unless a later explicit canon decision does so. For present purposes her Japanese family background remains fictional.

## 1603 portrayal rule

Maria must not read as intellectually helpless because she lacks English. In the Pelican she is frightened and physically endangered, but she is also observing the room, Rook, exits, alliances, gestures, and changes in attention. Her problem is lack of leverage and shared language, not lack of understanding.

The revised Chapter One should therefore show that she recognises Rook's account is losing support before Tom fully recognises it. Her restraint should be active: she chooses when to gesture, when to speak, when to withhold, and when to move.
