# The Venture — `The Same Ink` Historical Check

Status: research control for the structural rewrite. Sources checked 2026-08-08.

## Finding 1 — Sir Thomas Smythe is the strongest historical anchor

Supported.

Smythe was the first governor of the East India Company and, from 1609, the first treasurer/leading officer of the Virginia Company of London. He held the Virginia post until 1619 and the EIC governorship for most of 1600–1621.

Editorial use: keep Tom's late recognition that two apparently different ventures are connected through the same London merchant leadership. Do not claim that Smythe literally signed both charters at their foot unless a specific document supports that wording. Better: Tom recognises Smythe's name on working Company papers under different titles.

Sources:
- Encyclopedia Virginia, `Sir Thomas Smythe (ca. 1558–1625)`
- Encyclopedia Virginia, `Virginia Company of London`

## Finding 2 — Virginia Company lottery

Supported.

The 1612 third charter explicitly authorised public lotteries. The company relied on lotteries after investor confidence and profitability weakened.

Editorial use: the Paul's churchyard lottery scene is historically plausible and should remain, but it does not need a narrator explanation equating the lottery wheel with the EIC machine.

Sources:
- Encyclopedia Virginia primary document, `Third Charter of Virginia (1612)`
- Historic Jamestowne, `Virginia Company`

## Finding 3 — Samuel Argall / `Treasurer` chronology

Needs correction.

Argall commanded Sir Robert Rich's ship `Treasurer` to Virginia in 1612. He was in Virginia through early 1614, travelled to England in spring 1614, and returned to Virginia in summer 1615. The current chapter's neat autumn-1614 departure on `Treasurer` under Argall is therefore too specific and not sufficiently supported.

Editorial use: make Tom's 1614/15 westward outbound ship unnamed or fictionalised. Argall may enter later where the record is firmer: he commanded the 1616 homeward voyage carrying Pocahontas's party and the Virginia-bound `George` in 1617.

Source:
- Encyclopedia Virginia, `Samuel Argall (bap. 1580–1626)`

## Finding 4 — Virginia's strict laws

Supported in general; exact offences require care.

`Lawes Divine, Morall and Martiall` were issued beginning in 1610–1611 and remained in force until 1619, with severe corporal and capital punishments. The current exact claim about hanging for stealing an ear of corn was not confirmed in the source check and should not be retained without a primary citation.

Editorial use: show the severity through a posted/read order or a punishment Tom witnesses, but avoid an unverified picturesque offence.

Sources:
- Encyclopedia Virginia, `Lawes Divine, Morall and Martiall`
- Encyclopedia Virginia primary documents on the 1612 laws

## Finding 5 — Tobacco timing

Supported with moderation.

John Rolfe was growing `Nicotiana tabacum` by July 1612; samples reached England in 1613; by 1617 approximately 20,000 pounds arrived in England. Tobacco could therefore be visibly becoming Virginia's viable cash crop in Tom's 1615 experience.

Editorial use: retain tobacco's emerging dominance, but avoid implying the mature later plantation economy already exists in full in 1615.

Sources:
- Encyclopedia Virginia, `Tobacco in Colonial Virginia`
- Encyclopedia Virginia, `John Rolfe (d. 1622)`

## Finding 6 — Uttamatomakkin's tally stick

Supported.

Uttamatomakkin/Tomocomo accompanied Pocahontas to England in 1616 and was tasked by Powhatan with estimating the English population. Accounts describe him using a notched stick before abandoning the attempt because the number was too great.

Editorial use: retain the tally-stick image because it genuinely belongs to the history and resonates naturally with Maggie's notches. Reduce narrator explanation. Stage it at or just after arrival in Plymouth rather than insisting on a precise shipboard moment unless needed.

Sources:
- Encyclopedia Virginia, `Pocahontas (d. 1617)`
- historical summaries of Uttamatomakkin/Tomocomo's tally task

## Finding 7 — Pocahontas / Rebecca Rolfe chronology

Supported.

The party travelled to England in 1616 under Sir Thomas Dale, with Samuel Argall commanding the ship. They landed at Plymouth and travelled to London. In March 1617 the party attempted to return aboard the `George`; Pocahontas became ill near Gravesend and died there, with burial on March 21, 1617.

Editorial use: the 1616 arrival, London promotional purpose and 1617 Gravesend death are sound anchors. Tom should remain peripheral. Avoid turning Pocahontas into a thematic cameo who exists mainly to teach Tom a lesson.

Sources:
- Encyclopedia Virginia, `Pocahontas (d. 1617)`
- Encyclopedia Virginia, `John Rolfe (d. 1622)`
- National Park Service / Historic Jamestowne background

## Finding 8 — Current letter continuity is internally contradictory

The current `The Same Ink` says Tom writes **and sends** Maggie letters from ports for years. The repository's dedicated `tom-unsent-letters.md` says seven letters are written and **none sent**, with Daniel Vale as the eighth letter carried through the Pelican door.

Editorial decision: preserve the unsent-letter design. Rewrite `The Same Ink` so any Virginia/Plymouth letter is written but retained. The insertion of the westward chapter also means the old dates/content of the seven-letter planning document must be updated later.

## Historical rewrite guardrail

The revised chapter should use real history as pressure on Tom, not as a guided tour. Named historical people should remain peripheral to his working life. The chapter's central discovery is not `Virginia was secretly the EIC`; it is that Tom changes ocean and company and still encounters the same London habits of capital, paper, mortality and distance.
