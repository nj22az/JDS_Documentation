# The Venture — Parallel Audit A: Opening Integrity

Status: diagnostic report only. No prose changes in this audit.

## Scope

Reviewed the Step 4 natural-opening rewrite against the immediately following 1603 material and Maria's early eastward arc. Governing aim: characters must behave as people before they behave as symbols.

## Overall verdict

The new Chapter One is structurally stronger than the previous court/trial opening. The Pelican now exists as a working tavern before Rook arrives, Rook's accusation is initially credible, Tom intervenes before certainty, and the room turns against Rook by accumulation rather than formal judgement.

The principal problem has moved downstream: Chapter Two still describes the old courtroom version of the night and therefore re-imports the mythology the new Chapter One removes.

## Priority findings

### A1 — Chapter Two contradicts the new opening

`06-1603-what-the-women-did-merged.md` still says the room "saved her life," "reasoned its way to the truth," almost hanged her, then "reasoned themselves honest," and later describes the room as holding truth while releasing its owner. These are remnants of the old tribunal structure.

Action: rewrite only the affected reflections. Maria should remember concrete acts: Tom taking the blow, Maggie stopping Rook's handling, men ceasing to support Rook, Bell dying, and Rook escaping. She should not convert the night into a doctrine of rooms or justice.

### A2 — Hendricks continuity conflict

The new Chapter One places Hendricks in the Pelican throughout the evening and has him attempt communication with Maria. Chapter Two currently says Hendricks came in after she was sent upstairs and that the river stairs are the first time she sees his face.

Action: retain Hendricks in Chapter One, because his limited language bridge is useful and natural. Rewrite Chapter Two so Maria recognises him as the quiet Dutchman from the room, but does not yet trust him.

### A3 — Maria Mori name migration

The new opening and the early eastward chapters still contain `Maria de Sousa`. That surname is now deprecated.

Action: use **Maria Mori** in all revised Book One material. Bell's ledger may read `Maria Mori — Portuguese servant — transfer pending`; the deliberate error is the classification `Portuguese servant`, not her name. The official English record may still reduce her to `foreign female, name uncertain`.

### A4 — Maria is too fully formed in the old Chapter Two prose

The existing Chapter Two gives 1603 Maria mature doctrines she only needs to acquire later: rooms as account-keepers, a private internal ledger, explicit ideas about entries and true accounts, and highly polished conclusions about institutions.

This weakens her arc because the woman at the teak desk already exists almost complete on the first morning.

Action: preserve her intelligence, restraint, memory, languages and tactical awareness, but reduce mature ledger philosophy. In 1603 she should observe precisely and decide carefully. The doctrine comes later from repeated experience.

### A5 — Mariko-inspired restraint should replace panic-language

Older material says she "stammered and pleaded in two languages." That no longer fits the approved character direction.

Action: physical fear is permitted; loss of dignity is not required. Maria Mori should try Portuguese deliberately, then another language if useful, stop when neither works, watch the room, and conserve speech. Her problem is that nobody possesses the right language and she lacks leverage, not that she becomes incoherent.

### A6 — Tom's introduction now works

Tom's first moral act is stronger because he steps between Rook and Maria before he knows whether she is innocent. Keep this.

His inherited debt, willingness to sign despite understanding its unfairness, and the later thimble wager establish contradiction rather than sainthood. This should remain the governing Tom register.

### A7 — Maggie is improved, but protect her from becoming the thesis voice again

The new Chapter One mostly gives Maggie practical authority: stop violence, feed people, clear the table, keep the dangerous page dry. This is better than making her a judge.

Action: in downstream revisions, avoid giving Maggie polished explanations of what Wapping, justice, paper or empire mean. Her ethics should emerge through decisions and exact questions.

### A8 — Rook is more credible

Rook now begins with a plausible accusation and loses support gradually. Keep the boots, weather, grip and timing, but do not restore a clean evidentiary sequence in revision.

The strongest mechanism is that no single clue proves the case. Rook recognises that the social balance has changed before anyone announces a conclusion.

### A9 — The Pelican is finally a place

The wet Thursday opening works because the people have practical reasons to be there: tide, damaged tar barrel, wager, work, warmth. Preserve this method whenever Wapping is reintroduced later.

## Required prose changes after synthesis

1. Replace the two remaining `de Sousa` uses in the Step 4 opening with `Mori`.
2. Rewrite the affected Chapter Two paragraphs that remember a court/trial/near-lynching.
3. Repair Hendricks's arrival/recognition continuity.
4. Recast Maria's first-night language behaviour as controlled rather than panicked.
5. Reduce premature ledger/account philosophy in Maria's internal narration.
6. Preserve the actual escape over the roof, Pieter/Joos/Hendricks route, and the movement toward Amsterdam.

## Pass condition

The opening sequence passes when Chapter One and Chapter Two can be read consecutively without the reader ever feeling that the Pelican became a tribunal, while Maria Mori still emerges as intelligent, self-possessed, observant and dangerous to underestimate.
