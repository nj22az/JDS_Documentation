# The Venture — Natural Revision Style & Character Reference

Status: proposed editorial reference for the final prose pass.

## Governing principle

The reader should encounter people living through history, not characters demonstrating the author's theory of history.

The novel may still have arguments. The argument should emerge from recurrence, consequence and contrast.

## Wapping / Pelican

The Pelican is a working riverside tavern, not a court, shrine, conscience or supernatural memory machine.

Render Wapping through:

- tide and stairs;
- mud and weather;
- timber, rope, tar, canvas and ship repair;
- pay, debt, credit and rent;
- multilingual port life;
- bodies returning from voyages changed;
- changing buildings, warehouses and traffic.

Avoid narrator declarations that Wapping is wise, just, ancient, moral or remembering.

## Maggie

Voice:
- practical;
- blunt;
- economical;
- dry humour;
- questions rather than speeches.

Her account language belongs to ordinary tavern life: paid, owed, tab, debt, keep, return, missing.

Do not make her explain empire, institutions or the book's thesis.

Her authority comes from what she does: feeds people, refuses coercion under her roof, remembers debts and names, keeps dangerous paper dry, knows when not to ask another question.

## Tom

Early vocabulary is maritime and physical:
- load;
- strain;
- rope;
- weather;
- watch;
- repair;
- sequence;
- who stands where when something parts.

Administrative language enters him gradually because he spends years inside Company systems.

His tragedy is competence, not corruption into a villain. He repeatedly makes things work better and must confront what the improved thing is for.

Do not repeatedly call this a machine in narration.

## Maria Mori

Core inspiration: restrained intelligence, Christian identity, aristocratic bearing, languages, controlled defiance and refusal to waste speech.

She is not a copy of any existing fictional character's biography or plot.

1603:
- frightened where fear is physically appropriate;
- never intellectually helpless;
- tests language deliberately;
- stops speaking when speech has no leverage;
- watches status, hands, doors, objects and who obeys whom.

Development:
- Dutch and paper literacy are learned;
- duplicate practice begins as a practical response to loss and alteration;
- doctrine forms only after repeated experience;
- by 1612 her formal precision is earned.

Later voice:
- exact;
- controlled;
- formal English;
- no wasted threats;
- separates what she can prove, infer, buy, require and merely want.

## Arthur

Numerical, qualifying, legally exact.

He should dislike statements that exceed evidence.

His development is not from good to evil. It is from a man who once uses numbers to expose a lie to a man whose exact language can make institutional harm stable and administratively defensible.

## Daniel

Exactness under trauma.

His prose and dialogue should become shorter when the body interrupts him.

Do not aestheticise trauma through elaborate metaphor immediately after sensory triggers.

The revised Amboyna arc distinguishes:
- what Daniel personally knows;
- what English propaganda claims;
- what Dutch records claim;
- what remains disputed.

## Hendricks

Sardonic, maritime, commercial, experienced.

He may see patterns early but must not function as an omniscient thesis-delivery system.

His Dutch identity matters particularly in 1626: Carter's genever gesture must not make national collective blame the novel's endorsed position.

## Carter

Not dishonest by default.

His danger is that commerce remains available to him even during genuine grief.

Do not explain `grief becomes use` every time it happens. Show the practical question arriving after the human one.

## Rook

Physical, plausible and socially shrewd enough to create a story people initially accept.

Avoid theatrical villain speeches.

He does not secretly cause documented historical catastrophes. He recognises violent systems and finds work inside them.

His Book One fate after return to Batavia custody is unresolved.

## Dialogue

No quotation marks remains the Book One convention.

Prefer interruption, incomplete answers and questions carrying subtext over aphoristic exchanges.

A line that sounds quotable should earn that quality by being rare.

## Repetition hierarchy

### Protect

- `Food first.`
- `That was not my question.`
- `One hand. One foot. Then the others.`
- `Make another.`
- `Till I'm back.`
- `The ships go out. The boys sign.`

### Use sparingly

- `Not love. Recognition.`
- `Not pride. Entry.` / `Not approval. Entry.`
- `grief becomes use`
- `both truths` / `both accounts remain open`
- `machine`
- explicit statements that copies are witnesses.

## Objects

The physical objects remain load-bearing:

- thimble;
- Bell's page;
- Daniel's page;
- Maria Mori's packet;
- pie;
- blue cotton;
- genever bottle;
- whistle;
- Pieter's coat/book;
- teak desk.

Rule: after an object performs an emotional action, inspect the following paragraph. If the paragraph explains what the action meant, presume the paragraph should be cut.

## Historical exposition

Prefer:
- a missing wage;
- a ration count;
- a customs category;
- a ship's muster;
- a cargo problem;
- a printed lottery;
- a factor's annotation;
- a body;
- a letter arriving late;
- a title on a working document.

Avoid narrator paragraphs announcing what an institution will become in a later century.

## Moral contradiction

Contradiction remains central, but the reader should usually hold it without being told there are `two truths`.

State both sides explicitly only at genuine pivots where scene alone cannot carry the tension.

## Ending

The ending does not need to prove the thesis.

Joan's thyme, the boy's contract, Tom leaving the thimble and `Goodnight, Maggie` are sufficient.
