# The Venture — Chapter Five Pacing Check

Status: structural finding only. No immediate rewrite required.

## Chapter reviewed

`11-1613-the-pay-table-merged.md` — consolidated Chapter Five, carrying Jack's first voyage, illness/weather sequence, the later pay-table dispute and Arthur's parallel clerical arc.

## Verdict

**Keep the consolidated chapter. Do not split it back into separate chapters.**

Its central movement is coherent: Jack begins as a debt attached to Tom, becomes a working sailor under Tom's voice, survives actual danger, and eventually becomes capable of refusing another voyage on his own terms. That movement belongs in one chapter.

The pacing problem is not architecture. It is density.

## Strong material to protect

- Jack carrying his own debt down the ladder.
- The stolen blanket and Tom refusing to solve every problem for him.
- First climb and `One hand. One foot. Then the others.`
- The worn buntline / Tom physically getting Jack off it.
- Jack learning the difference between wage accounting and being physically fed.
- The flux sequence and nineteen names.
- The Madagascar/squall sequence, including Jack cutting Tom free.
- Jack's damaged hand/fingers and later wage reckoning.
- Tom forcing the missing wages back out of the pay system.
- Jack choosing not to sail again: `Where I can see both ends of the rope.`
- Arthur's parallel development from Bell's chair into the man who can make harm orderly.

## Compression target

The chapter spends too long proving that shipboard life is detailed and material after that has already been established.

Highest-value compression area: the extended middle of `The Voice`, where ration issue, beer, water, deck scrubbing, jokes, serving rope, food and heat each receive full scenes before the flux begins.

These details are good individually. Together they delay the next irreversible event.

Recommended final prose pass:

- retain the ration-book conflict because it develops Jack/Tom and the book's institutional theme through action;
- retain one comic belonging beat (beetles or oakum tail, not necessarily both at full length);
- retain one maintenance lesson (`Find the join` / serving the shroud);
- compress the remaining routine into connective prose;
- enter the flux earlier.

Estimated desirable reduction from the shipboard-routine portion: **15–25%**, without cutting the danger, illness, squall or pay-table movements.

## Why not split

A split would weaken the causal payoff. The pay-table confrontation matters because the reader has lived through what Jack's wage actually cost his body. Separating voyage and reckoning into different chapters would make the accounting feel more abstract.

## Pass condition

The chapter should leave the reader feeling that a long voyage has occurred without requiring the reader to experience every representative day of it.
