## Remove / recast old legal language

### Notches

Retire:
- `A cut would have been the verdict.`
- any description of notches as verdicts.

A cut records that the house has stopped honestly expecting the man to return. Nothing more.

### `Counter-ledger`

The phrase may survive as an **editorial label**, but the narration should not repeatedly call the objects an alternative account system.

Cut/recast:
- `The counter-ledger does not pretend to the Company's exactness.`
- `An honest blank is still an entry.`
- `A stool can be an entry.`
- `Paper is not the only material that can hold an account.`
- similar explanatory formulations around the fault.

Let Maggie keep objects because people left them, families may claim them, and Bell's page is dangerous. The reader can recognise the pattern later.

### The fault

The physical details are excellent:
- dry oak;
- green shadow of brass;
- salt cleaning;
- splinter left because too-smooth hiding place looks made;
- temporary objects closest to hand.

Protect these. They make the hiding place believable without metaphysics.

### Barnaby's stool

Keep the stool and the stranger being told `Taken — Barnaby.`

Cut the explanatory paragraph that says the room has begun keeping things outside the fault. The stool itself is sufficient.

### Bar extension

Keep the joiner preserving the old length.

Cut the Company-versus-Maggie explanatory comparison after it. `Six more men she can feed` can remain if spoken/held close to Maggie, but do not convert a carpentry job into a formal thesis statement.

## 1611–1612 chronology

Tom's blue-cotton material occurs before his 1612 return and remains valid.

The revised Chapter Six later handles the 1614 Virginia choice. Do not insert Virginia foreshadowing here beyond Tom's existing dissatisfaction/competence.

## 1612 rupture

Use the new insert exactly as the structural basis:
- Jack has agency;
- Tom has genuinely improved Jack's odds;
- Maggie has no clean alternative;
- her objection is Tom using inevitability to cleanse his own participation;
- thimble is set down, not theatrically thrown;
- Maggie may be partly wrong;
- neither character knows how long the rupture will last.

## Epigraph

`The ships keep the wage. I keep the leaving.` is close to the old ledger philosophy but remains grounded enough in tavern practice to be considered during final prose. It is not mandatory.

## Pass condition

The chapter should leave the reader noticing, only gradually, that Maggie's house has accumulated objects and memories the official paperwork lacks. Maggie herself need not conceptualise this as a competing archival system.
